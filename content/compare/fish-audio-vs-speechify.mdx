If you are picking between [Fish Audio](/reviews/fish-audio) and [Speechify](/reviews/speechify), the honest answer is: they are not competitors. [Fish Audio](https://fish.audio) is a developer platform for building voice into apps. [Speechify](https://speechify.com) is a consumer app for listening to text. Comparing them is like comparing a kitchen to a restaurant, both involve food, but you use them for completely different things.

We tested both tools across their actual use cases: API integration and voice production for Fish Audio, reading workflow and accessibility for Speechify. Here is how they compare across the dimensions that really matter.

## Developer Platform vs Consumer App

[Fish Audio](/reviews/fish-audio) is a developer platform. Its core product is an API that converts text to speech with sub-150ms latency, voice cloning from 10 seconds of audio, and emotion control via natural language tags. You integrate it into your own app, game, or workflow. 26,000+ GitHub stars, open-source model weights you can download and run locally, and pricing at roughly $0.05 per minute for English, about 70% cheaper than ElevenLabs.

[Speechify](/reviews/speechify) is a consumer product. Its core product is an app and Chrome extension that reads text aloud to you, web articles, PDFs, emails, even physical book pages via OCR camera. 200+ HD voices including celebrity options, smooth sync across iOS, Android, Web, Mac, and Windows. Built by a dyslexic founder, won an Apple Design Award in 2025 for accessibility.

**Winner: Neither.** You do not choose between these tools, you choose based on what you are building versus what you are consuming.

## Voice Quality and Flexibility

On raw voice quality benchmarks, Fish Audio's S2-Pro model with 4B parameters and 10M+ training hours ranks #1 on TTS-Arena, ahead of ElevenLabs, OpenAI, and Google. The emotion control system with 15,000+ natural language tags like `[whispering urgently]` and `[laughing nervously]` lets you direct voice performance inline in your script. For developers generating dynamic content, this level of control is unmatched.

Speechify's 200+ HD voices win on variety and accessibility. Licensed celebrity voices like Snoop Dogg and Gwyneth Paltrow are a nice marketing touch, but the real advantage is having 200 different reading voices to pick from, you can find one that does not grate after 3 hours of listening. Voice quality at 1x to 2.5x speed is excellent for a reading app. Above 3x, things get compressed and unnatural.

The key difference: Fish Audio voices are designed to be indistinguishable from human speech for production use. Speechify voices are designed to be pleasant and clear for long listening sessions. Different optimization targets, different winners.

**Winner: Fish Audio** for production voice quality and control. **Speechify** for consumer listening variety and endurance.

## Pricing

|  | Fish Audio | Speechify |
|--|----------|----------|
| **Free** | 8,000 credits/month, no commercial rights | ~10 standard voices, basic playback |
| **Entry** | $5.50-11/month Plus (200 min) | $139/year Premium ($11.58/mo) |
| **Best value** | $37.50-75/month Pro (1,620 min) | Premium Annual at $139/year |
| **Self-hosted** | Free model weights, needs 12-24GB GPU | N/A |

Fish Audio's self-hosting option is a pricing wildcard. If you have a 24GB GPU and technical skills, you can run S2-Pro locally at zero per-request cost. Commercial use requires a license, but the math still works out dramatically cheaper than any API-based alternative at scale.

Speechify's pricing is straightforward: either you pay $139/year or you do not. The monthly option at $29/month is punitive, $348/year for the same features. The free tier is genuinely useful as a trial but the robotic-sounding voices will push most people to upgrade within a week.

Fish Audio's UTF-8 byte billing is the main pricing gotcha. Non-Latin scripts like Chinese, Japanese, and Arabic cost 3-4x more per word because each character uses more bytes. Speechify's pricing does not discriminate by language.

- People with dyslexia, ADHD, or visual impairments

- Commuters who want to turn 30+ minutes of daily reading into listening time
- Anyone who wants a polished, cross-platform consumer app, not an API

## FAQ

**Can Speechify replace Fish Audio for voiceover production?**

No. Speechify Studio at $19-49/month does voice cloning and dubbing, but it is a separate product aimed at creators, and quality trails both [ElevenLabs](/reviews/elevenlabs) and [Fish Audio](/reviews/fish-audio) for professional use. Speechify's core product is for consuming text, not producing voiceovers.

**Can Fish Audio do what Speechify does for reading?**

Not really. Fish Audio has no consumer reading app, no Chrome extension for web articles, no OCR scanner for physical books. It outputs audio via API, you would need to build your own reading app around it. Technically possible but not practical for personal use.

**Which is cheaper for heavy usage?**

Fish Audio at scale, especially self-hosted. Speechify is a fixed $139/year regardless of usage, which is fantastic value if you listen to hundreds of hours per year. But if you are generating 100+ hours of audio monthly via API, Fish Audio's $0.05/min rate or free self-hosting wins by miles.

**Do I need both?**

Honestly, possibly yes, if you are a developer who also reads a lot. Fish Audio for building and generating, Speechify for personal reading and consuming. They occupy completely non-overlapping slots in a workflow.
