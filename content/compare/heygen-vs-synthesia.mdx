If you're picking between [HeyGen](/reviews/heygen) and [Synthesia](/reviews/synthesia), the honest answer is: it depends on whether you're shipping personalized sales videos or scaling internal training. Both tools own the AI avatar market, but they've grown into different shapes — and the wrong pick wastes a year of subscription budget before you notice.

We tested both across actual production scenarios: localization, training modules, sales outreach, and product demos. Here's what holds up, what breaks, and which team should pick which.

## Avatar Quality and Realism

This is where the two tools have actually diverged. Synthesia's avatars look like a studio shot — clean lighting, controlled posture, professional but a bit static. HeyGen's avatars look more like a person on a Zoom call — slightly less polished, but with more natural micro-expressions and head movement.

Which is better depends on context. For a corporate training module on data privacy, Synthesia's "trustworthy spokesperson" energy is what you want. For a 30-second LinkedIn outreach video to a sales prospect, HeyGen's casual realism converts better — we've seen reply rates 2-3x higher than scripted text emails.

The bigger gap: **custom avatars**. HeyGen lets you create one from a 2-minute selfie video shot on your phone. Synthesia requires either a studio recording session or their pre-made stock library for most plans. If you need every salesperson on the team to have their own avatar, HeyGen wins this on logistics alone.

**Winner: HeyGen** for personalization, **Synthesia** for stock professional quality.

## Localization and Multilingual Output

Both tools translate scripts and lip-sync to the new language. Both support 140+ languages. The differences are in the details.

Synthesia's translation tooling is built around the "create once, deploy everywhere" use case — you write a master script, click a button, get the video in 30 languages with consistent avatar performance across all of them. The output quality is uniformly good. This is why companies use it for training content that needs to land identically in Tokyo and São Paulo.

HeyGen's localization is more aggressive about preserving the source video's energy. If your English original has the salesperson laughing at minute 0:42, the Spanish version still laughs at minute 0:42, with matching mouth shape. It's uncanny in the right way for marketing — and slightly creepy if you're not used to it.

For pure language coverage, HeyGen's 175 vs Synthesia's 140 isn't usually decisive. The lower-tier languages on HeyGen are noticeably worse than the top 30. Same is true for Synthesia's lower tier.

**Winner: Synthesia** for translation consistency at scale, **HeyGen** for preserving source-video personality.

## FAQ

**Is HeyGen better than Synthesia?** Not categorically — they're built for different jobs. HeyGen wins on personalization speed and casual realism. Synthesia wins on enterprise polish and translation consistency. Pick based on use case, not feature counts.

**Can you use both HeyGen and Synthesia for free?** HeyGen has a real free tier (3 watermarked videos/month). Synthesia only offers a 36-second free trial — you'll need to commit to a paid plan to test it properly.

**Which has better lip-sync quality?** Roughly equivalent on English. Synthesia is slightly more consistent across languages. HeyGen handles fast-paced, expressive scripts better; Synthesia handles formal, measured delivery better.

**Do either offer real-time avatar generation?** Neither does true real-time. Both render videos from scripts in 1-5 minutes depending on length and resolution. For real-time avatar use cases, you'll need a different category of tool entirely.

**Can I switch from one to the other later?** Yes, but custom avatars don't transfer. If you've already paid to create studio avatars on Synthesia, switching to HeyGen means starting that work over.
