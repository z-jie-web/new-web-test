[Stable Audio](https://stableaudio.com) and [AIVA](https://aiva.ai) are both AI music generators, but calling them competitors is like calling a table saw and a scalpel competing cutting tools. Stable Audio generates royalty-free loops, stems, and sound effects for producers who build tracks from components. AIVA generates orchestral and cinematic compositions for film, games, and commercial scoring. They share a category label — "AI music" — and almost nothing else.

## Output Type: Fundamentally Different Products

Stable Audio generates building blocks. Ask it for a 30-second drum loop in boom-bap style, and you get exactly that — a loop you can drop into Ableton, Logic, or FL Studio. Ask for a 45-second ambient pad, a 2-minute bass line, or a pack of risers and transitions for video editing. It's designed for producers who assemble tracks from components, not for people who want a finished song.

AIVA generates complete orchestral compositions. Describe a scene — "epic battle, 120 BPM, D minor, building tension" — and AIVA writes a full piece with strings, brass, percussion, and dynamics that follow the emotional arc. It's a composer, not a loop library. You get finished pieces suitable for film scores, game soundtracks, and commercial use.

If you produce electronic music and need stems for your DAW: Stable Audio.
If you score film or game scenes and need orchestral pieces: AIVA.
These workflows don't overlap.

**Winner: No contest — different tools for different jobs.**

## Copyright and Licensing: Both Win, Differently

Stable Audio's legal standing is the cleanest in AI music. It's trained on the [AudioSparx](https://www.audiosparx.com) licensed library — no scraped data, no fair-use gray area, no pending lawsuits. All paid tiers include full commercial license. You can use Stable Audio output in YouTube videos, podcasts, ads, and games without worrying about takedowns. This matters if you're a content creator whose revenue depends on not getting copyright-striked.

AIVA takes a different approach: full copyright ownership on the Pro plan (€49/month, ~$53). You own 100% of the IP rights — no royalty sharing, no attribution required, no licensing complications. This is unique in the AI music space. No other tool grants full copyright. For commercial music production where IP ownership is a contract requirement (film, games, advertising), AIVA's Pro plan is the only option that delivers.

The catch: AIVA's full copyright requires the €49/month Pro plan. The cheaper Standard plan (€15/month) grants a commercial license but not full ownership. Stable Audio's commercial license kicks in at $11.99/month.

**Winner: Stable Audio** for budget-friendly commercial licensing. **AIVA** for full IP ownership.

## MIDI Export: AIVA's Unfair Advantage

AIVA exports MIDI files on Standard plan and above. This means you can load AIVA's compositions into your DAW (Logic Pro, Ableton, Cubase) and edit every note — change the orchestration, adjust timing, swap instruments, add your own parts. MIDI turns AIVA from a finished-product generator into a composition assistant that gives you a professional starting point.

Stable Audio has no MIDI export. You get audio files — WAV or MP3. If you want to change a note or swap an instrument, you regenerate the whole thing and hope the next version is better.

For producers and composers who work in DAWs, MIDI export is the difference between "useful starting point" and "locked audio I can't edit." AIVA wins this by default — Stable Audio doesn't compete here.

**Winner: AIVA** — MIDI export fundamentally changes how you work with AI-generated music in a DAW.

## Genre Versatility

Stable Audio handles electronic production well — hip-hop beats, ambient textures, house loops, trap hi-hats, lo-fi chords. It's trained on a broad music library and produces usable results across electronic and beat-driven genres. What it can't do: orchestral, acoustic instruments with realistic articulation, or anything that needs to sound like human musicians playing together.

AIVA is the opposite. It's exceptional at orchestral, cinematic, and classical — the kind of music you'd hear in a Christopher Nolan film or a Final Fantasy game. It's trained on 8+ years of classical music (Mozart, Beethoven, Bach) and has preset styles for film scoring moods. What it can't do: anything modern. No pop, no hip-hop, no electronic, no beats.

If your work spans both electronic production and orchestral scoring, you need both tools. Neither replaces the other.

**Winner: Depends entirely on your genre.** Stable Audio for electronic. AIVA for orchestral.

## FAQ

**Can Stable Audio generate full songs?**
No. Stable Audio generates loops, stems, and sound effects up to 3 minutes. It's designed for producers who build tracks from components in a DAW, not for people who want finished songs. If you want full songs with vocals, [Suno](/compare/suno-vs-udio) is the better choice.

**Does AIVA sound like real orchestral music?**
Yes, for the price point. AIVA's orchestral output is noticeably better than Suno or Udio in the classical/cinematic space — proper string articulation, realistic brass swells, and dynamics that follow emotional arcs. It won't replace the London Symphony Orchestra, but for background scoring, YouTube content, and game audio, the quality is professional enough.

**Can I use Stable Audio music on YouTube without copyright issues?**
Yes, on paid tiers. Stable Audio's commercial license covers YouTube, podcasts, ads, and social media. Since it's trained on licensed AudioSparx data, there's no fair-use gray area. This is the safest option if you want to avoid Content ID claims.

**Is AIVA's copyright ownership worth €49/month?**
If you're producing music for commercial distribution (streaming, sync licensing, games, film) where IP ownership is a contract requirement — yes, absolutely. If you're a hobbyist or YouTuber who just needs background music — no, the Standard plan at €15/month with commercial licensing is sufficient.

**Can I use both Stable Audio and AIVA together?**
Many producers do. Stable Audio for electronic production elements (drums, bass, synths) and AIVA for orchestral layers and cinematic scoring. The combination covers both modern and classical production at a combined cost of ~$28/month ($12 + €15).

**Which is better for game audio?**
Depends on the game. Stable Audio for adaptive loops (background music that loops seamlessly, UI sounds, ambient textures). AIVA for narrative scoring (boss battle themes, cutscene music, emotional story moments). Many game studios use both.
