[Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com) and [Quillbot](https://quillbot.com) get compared constantly because they both sit under "AI writing tools." But they were built for different jobs. Grammarly is an editing layer that catches errors, tightens clarity, and adjusts tone across the apps where you already type. Quillbot is a paraphrasing engine that rewrites existing text through multiple modes — Standard, Fluency, Academic, Creative, and more — while preserving meaning. One fixes what you wrote. The other gives you alternative ways to say it. Read our full [Grammarly review](/reviews/grammarly) and [Quillbot review](/reviews/quillbot) if you need the deep dive on either tool. This page focuses on which one fits your actual workflow. For user sentiment outside the marketing pages, Quillbot's [Product Hunt page](https://www.producthunt.com/products/quillbot) and The Verge's [ongoing AI coverage](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence) of tools like Grammarly are useful third-party references.

## Key Differences

### Grammar Safety Net vs. Paraphrasing Engine

Grammarly's value is in catching problems before they leave your screen. It underlines awkward phrasing in Gmail, flags passive voice in Google Docs, and suggests clearer alternatives in Slack. You do not open Grammarly for a "writing session." It is already there, running in the background, making your draft cleaner as you type. That is why it remains the default editing tool for millions of professionals: it removes friction rather than adding a step to the workflow.

Quillbot's value is in giving you options. Paste a paragraph, pick Academic mode, and it rewrites the passage with elevated vocabulary and formal sentence structure. Switch to Creative mode, and it reimagines the same idea with more varied language. This is useful when you know what you want to say but not how to phrase it — common for non-native English speakers, students drafting papers, and anyone staring at a sentence that feels "off" but cannot pinpoint why.

The difference in practice: Grammarly tells you "this sentence has a clarity problem, here is a fix." Quillbot tells you "here are 8 different ways to say the same thing, pick one."

### Where They Work Best

Grammarly works best when you are already writing and want a safety net. Emails, reports, client proposals, slide notes, Slack messages — any workday writing that needs to be cleaner before you hit send. Its strength is breadth: it works across browsers, Google Docs, Microsoft Word, and desktop apps. You do not switch contexts to use it.

Quillbot works best when you have a draft and need to rework it. Students pasting a rough paragraph into the editor and running it through Academic mode. Researchers rewording a literature review section to avoid accidental plagiarism. Non-native speakers checking whether a sentence sounds natural in English. Quillbot is a destination you visit when the job is rewriting, not a layer that follows you through the day.

## Pricing Comparison

| Plan | Grammarly | Quillbot |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Free** | Basic grammar, spelling, limited clarity | 125 words per paraphrase, Standard + Fluency modes, basic grammar check |
| **Paid entry** | $12/month annually (Pro) | $9.95/month (Premium, $4.17/month on annual) |
| **Full suite** | $12/month (everything bundled) | $18.28/month (Premium + Plagiarism add-on) |
| **Enterprise** | Custom | Not available |

Grammarly bundles everything into one Pro price — grammar, clarity, tone, plagiarism checker, and full rewrite suggestions. There is no separate add-on for any feature.

Quillbot splits its offering. Premium at $9.95/month gives you all 8 paraphrasing modes and unlimited usage. The plagiarism checker costs an extra $8.33/month. So the full Quillbot suite runs $18.28/month versus Grammarly's $12/month. That math only matters if you need the plagiarism checker — most students already have Turnitin access through their university, making the Premium-only tier the practical pick.

On the free tier: Grammarly's free version catches basic grammar and spelling across all your apps. It is enough to decide whether you need the paid upgrade. Quillbot's free tier limits you to 125 words per paraphrase in two modes — useful for quick rewording of a paragraph or two, but frustrating if you need to rework a full paper.

## Who Should Choose Grammarly

Grammarly is the right pick if you edit daily across many writing surfaces. Professionals who send dozens of emails, write client proposals, draft reports, and post in Slack all day get the most value because Grammarly improves all of those outputs without any extra clicks. If your writing workflow is scattered across 5 different apps and you want one editing layer that follows you through all of them, Grammarly is the tool for that job.

Pick Grammarly if:
- You write for work across email, docs, and presentations every day
- You want grammar, clarity, and tone help inside the apps you already use
- You care more about catching errors than rephrasing ideas
- You want one price that covers the full editing suite

## Who Should Choose Quillbot

Quillbot is the right pick if your main task is rewording existing text. Students writing papers who need to paraphrase sources without plagiarizing. Researchers formatting literature reviews and citation lists. Non-native English speakers who have good ideas but want help expressing them more naturally. Anyone who pastes text into a tool and thinks "I need this to sound different" is the Quillbot user.

Pick Quillbot if:
- You are a student or researcher who needs plagiarism-safe paraphrasing
- You need multiple ways to phrase the same idea without starting from scratch
- You want a citation generator and grammar checker built into the same platform
- You work in multiple languages and need paraphrasing support beyond English

## FAQ

**Can I use Grammarly and Quillbot together?**
Yes, and many people do. A common workflow: write your draft, run it through Grammarly to catch grammar and clarity issues, then paste sections into Quillbot when you want alternative phrasings for specific sentences. Grammarly handles the editing layer. Quillbot handles the rewording layer. They do not compete; they stack.

**Which is better for academic writing?**
Quillbot. Its Academic mode is purpose-built for scholarly writing conventions — elevated vocabulary, formal register, passive-to-active restructuring. The citation generator saves 20-30 minutes per paper on bibliography formatting alone. Grammarly is useful for catching errors in academic writing, but it was not designed specifically for academic paraphrasing workflows.

**Which is better for professional writing?**
Grammarly. It works across the apps professionals already use — Gmail, Google Docs, Microsoft Word, Slack — and catches the errors that get you in trouble in workplace writing: tone mismatches, unclear phrasing, overly complex sentences. Quillbot requires you to paste text into its editor, which adds a step that most professionals will skip after the first week.

**Do I need the paid version of either tool?**
Grammarly's free tier handles basic grammar and spelling well enough for casual use. The Pro tier becomes worth it when you write for work daily and the tone and clarity suggestions save real editing time. Quillbot's free tier at 125 words per paraphrase is fine for occasional use but too limited for writing a full paper or report. If you are a student with one or two papers per semester, the free tier might be enough. If you write regularly, the Premium plan at $4.17/month (annual) is the practical entry point.
