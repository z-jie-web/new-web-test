Mubert and Soundraw both sell royalty-free AI music for content creators, but they solve different problems. Mubert is a background music service: pick a mood, hit play, and get continuous streaming audio that is safe for YouTube and Twitch. Soundraw is a beat production tool: pick genre, mood, tempo, and instruments, then export individual stems to edit in your DAW. If your priority is never worrying about a copyright strike on your stream, Mubert is the safer bet. If your priority is customizing every element of a beat before it hits your timeline, Soundraw gives you more control.

## Key Differences

### Continuous Streaming vs. One-Shot Beat Generation

Mubert generates music as a continuous stream. You set your parameters (genre, mood, duration) and the engine plays audio that adapts in real time. Change the mood mid-stream and the music transitions smoothly. This model is built for live streaming and long-form content where the music needs to run for 30 minutes or more without interruption.

Soundraw generates individual beats in about 10 seconds per batch. You get 5-10 variations per generation, preview them, pick the best one, and either download it or open it in the Beat-Maker to tweak the structure. This model is built for editors who need a specific 2-minute track for a video intro or a 30-second loop for a TikTok.

The practical difference: if you run a 3-hour Twitch stream with changing energy levels (chill gameplay, then boss fight, then chat segment), Mubert follows the vibe shift automatically. If you are editing a 10-minute YouTube video and need a specific beat that rises during the climax, Soundraw gives you the control to build that exact track.

### Licensing Confidence vs. Creative Customization

Mubert's licensing is its strongest feature. Paid plans (Creator at $14/month) include a commercial license that explicitly covers YouTube and Twitch streaming. The platform guarantees no Content ID claims and no copyright strikes. For creators who have been burned by automated copyright detection, this legal clarity matters more than any feature.

Soundraw also offers royalty-free licensing on paid plans, but its real advantage is the creative toolkit. STEM downloads on the Creator plan ($29.99/month) export individual instrument tracks (drums, bass, melody, synths, FX) that you load into Ableton, Logic Pro, or FL Studio. Replace the AI bass with your own recording. Add live guitar over the AI drums. Remix the melody with different effects. Soundraw treats its output as raw material for production. Mubert treats its output as a finished product.

The Beat-Maker reinforces this difference. You drag song sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro) to design the structure of your track. You control the energy arc. Mubert does not offer this level of structural editing because it assumes you want the music to play continuously in the background.

### Genre and Style Coverage

Mubert stays in the electronic lane: ambient, downtempo, house, techno, drum and bass, lo-fi hip-hop, synthwave. These are the genres that work as background music for videos and streams, and Mubert does them well. But it does not attempt hip-hop with 808s, jazz with walking bass lines, acoustic folk, or cinematic orchestration.

Soundraw covers more ground: hip-hop, lo-fi, pop, electronic, trap, cinematic, R&B, reggae, jazz, funk, rock. The beats still lean electronic (you are not getting a live jazz quartet recording), but the genre menu is broader. For creators who work across different content styles and want matching beats for each, Soundraw provides more variety.

Neither tool generates vocals. For full songs with singing, [Suno](/reviews/suno) is the tool to look at.

## Who Should Choose Mubert

Pick Mubert if you stream on Twitch or publish on YouTube and background music licensing keeps you up at night. The streaming-safe guarantee (explicit coverage for YouTube and Twitch with no Content ID risk) is the reason to choose Mubert over any other AI music tool.

Mubert also makes sense if you want music that adapts to mood changes in real time. Streamers who switch between calm and high-energy segments get music that follows the vibe without manually queuing tracks. Video editors who use Mubert Render get a soundtrack automatically synced to their edit duration, which saves time compared to browsing stock libraries for a track that happens to match.

Choose Mubert if:
- You need continuous background music for streams or long-form content
- You want a tool that requires zero music production knowledge. Pick a genre and mood, hit play
- Licensing confidence is your top priority, not creative control
- You work primarily with electronic music genres and do not need stems

Choose Soundraw if:

## FAQ

**Can I use Mubert and Soundraw music on YouTube without copyright claims?**

Yes, on paid plans for both tools. Mubert explicitly guarantees YouTube and Twitch streaming safety with no Content ID claims. Soundraw provides a royalty-free commercial license. Both generate original audio from their own training data, so there is no risk of matching copyrighted recordings in Content ID scans.

**Which tool is better for a streamer who needs hours of background music?**

Mubert. The real-time streaming engine generates continuous music that adapts to mood changes without manual track switching. You set the genre and mood once, and the music runs for the entire stream. Soundraw generates individual 2-4 minute beats; you would need to queue them manually or loop them, which is more work for live streaming.

**Can I edit the music from either tool in my DAW?**

Only Soundraw exports individual stems for DAW editing, and only on the $29.99/month Creator plan. You get separate tracks for drums, bass, melody, synths, and FX that load into Ableton, Logic Pro, FL Studio, or any DAW. Mubert exports stereo MP3 or WAV files. You can drop the stereo file into a DAW timeline, but you cannot isolate or replace individual instruments.

**Do either of these tools generate vocals?**

No. Both Mubert and Soundraw are instrumental-only. Mubert focuses on electronic background music. Soundraw focuses on beats and instrumentals across multiple genres. If you need songs with singing, [Suno](/reviews/suno) generates full vocal tracks from text prompts.

**Which is better value for a YouTuber who posts 2-3 videos per week?**

Mubert at $14/month gives you unlimited downloads, commercial licensing, and the Mubert Render feature that syncs music to video duration automatically. For a YouTuber who just needs background tracks that are safe to monetize, Mubert covers everything at the lower price. Soundraw at $16.99/month (with stem exports requiring the $29.99/month tier) only makes sense if you plan to edit beats in a DAW before using them.
