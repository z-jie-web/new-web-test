Most bloggers use AI wrong. They ask ChatGPT to write a full blog post, get back a generic wall of text, and spend as much time editing as they would have spent writing from scratch.

The right workflow is different: AI handles the mechanical parts (outline, first draft, grammar), and you handle the creative parts (angle, anecdotes, voice). When you split the work this way, you can publish 3x faster without any detectable drop in quality.

I have written 43 blog posts on this site using this exact workflow. Here is how it works.

## The Three-Phase Workflow

### Phase 1: Outline (10 minutes)

Do not ask AI to "write a blog post about X." Start with the outline. You control the structure. AI helps you avoid staring at a blank page.

**What you do:**
1. Write down your core argument in one sentence. If you cannot do this, you are not ready to write yet.
2. List the 3-5 main points that support your argument.
3. Define your target reader — who are you writing for?

**What AI does:**
Take your core argument and main points, and ask [Claude](/reviews/claude) or [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt) to expand them into a detailed outline with sub-points and section headers.

**Prompt template:**
> I am writing a blog post about [topic]. My core argument is [one sentence]. My main points are [bullet list]. My target reader is [audience]. Generate a detailed outline with section headers and sub-points that support my argument. Keep it conversational, not academic.

This takes about 2 minutes. You now have a complete skeleton.

### Phase 2: Draft (20-30 minutes)

Now you write the draft. But instead of staring at each section and trying to fill it, use AI to write one section at a time.

**What you do:**
- Paste one section from your outline at a time
- Give the AI your voice guidelines (tone, vocabulary, what to avoid)
- Review each section as it comes out, tweak the prompt if it drifts

**What AI does:**
Write the first draft of each section based on your outline and voice guidelines. Do not ask it to write the entire post at once — the output will be worse and harder to edit.

**Section-by-section prompt template:**
> Write a [word count] section for a blog post. Topic: [section header from outline]. Voice: conversational, short paragraphs, no jargon, include specific examples. Target reader: [audience]. Key points to cover: [bullet points for this section].

**Which AI tool for drafting:**
- [Claude](/reviews/claude) for long-form content where voice consistency matters. Output reads more naturally over 500+ words.
- For fast generation and multi-format output (you can turn sections into social posts later), use [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt).
- For structured, SEO-focused content with specific templates, use [Jasper](/reviews/jasper).

### Phase 3: Edit (15-20 minutes)

This is where you turn AI output into your writing. Do not skip this step.

**What to edit:**
1. **Add personal anecdotes.** AI cannot write about your experience. Add 1-2 specific examples from your own work.
2. **Cut AI-isms.** Remove phrases like "in today's digital landscape," "delve into," "comprehensive overview." Replace them with conversational alternatives.
3. **Check facts.** AI can invent statistics and cite sources that do not exist. Verify every factual claim.
4. **Optimize for your reader.** Add context your specific audience needs. Remove content that is too basic or too advanced.
5. **Add internal links.** AI does not know your other posts. Add 2-3 links to your related content.
6. **Grammar check.** Run the final draft through [Grammarly](/reviews/grammarly) for one last polish pass.

The edited output should read like you wrote it from scratch, just faster.

## Common Mistakes That Slow You Down

**1. Using one prompt for the entire post.**
A single prompt produces a template-style article with formulaic structure. Readers notice. Generate section by section instead.

**2. Skipping the outline.**
Without an outline, the AI structures the post for you. The result is generic because the AI defaults to a safe, predictable structure. Your outline gives the post a unique shape.

**3. Over-editing.**
You do not need to rewrite every sentence. If a paragraph is factually correct and reads naturally, keep it. Focus your editing energy on sections where the AI missed your voice.

**4. Using the same AI tool for everything.**
Claude is better for long-form voice consistency. Grammarly is better for grammar. ChatGPT is better for multi-format repurposing. Mix tools based on what each phase needs.

**5. Not keeping your own voice examples.**
Save 2-3 paragraphs that best represent your writing voice. Paste these into the AI prompt as voice references. This one habit improves output quality more than any prompt engineering trick.

## FAQ

**Does Google penalize AI-written blog posts?**
Google has stated that AI content is acceptable if it provides genuine value. The key word is "genuine." A blog post that is entirely AI-generated with no editing provides minimal value because it is generic. A blog post that uses AI for drafting but includes original examples, anecdotes, and voice provides value. Google's quality algorithms are good at detecting which is which.

**Which AI tool produces the most human-sounding blog posts?**
Claude. Its output is less formulaic, uses fewer recognizable AI patterns, and maintains a consistent voice over longer articles. ChatGPT is faster but produces more predictable text patterns that experienced editors can spot. See our [Claude vs ChatGPT for blog writing](/blog/claude-vs-chatgpt-for-writing-blog-posts) comparison.

**Can I produce more than one post per day with this workflow?**
Yes. Once you internalize the three-phase workflow, you can run 2-3 posts per day by batching phases: outline all three posts in the morning session, draft them all mid-day, and edit them all in the evening. The bottleneck becomes your editing speed, not the AI's generation speed.

**Should I disclose AI use to my readers?**
For general blog content, most readers do not care how the post was produced — they care whether it is useful. For content where authenticity matters (personal stories, opinion pieces, investigative journalism), AI use should be minimal or disclosed. Some publications have specific AI disclosure policies.
