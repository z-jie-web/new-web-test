They're all "AI music," but they solve completely different problems, and choosing the wrong one wastes hours.

## 1. Suno, Best for Full Songs with Vocals

[Read full Suno review →](/reviews/suno)

**What it's good at:** Speed and completeness. Suno v5.5 generates a full song, two verses, chorus, bridge, outro with vocals and instrumentation, in under 30 seconds. In our test, we typed "upbeat pop song about quitting your job to start a company, female vocals, synth-forward, 120 BPM" and got a track that sounded like something from a Spotify indie playlist. The built-in DAW (Suno Studio) with 12-stem separation lets you isolate the vocal, swap the drums, or re-record the bridge without leaving the browser. Voice cloning on the Premier tier ($30/month) trains on 30-60 seconds of your singing voice. The output captures your timbre and delivery style, not perfect on sibilance and vibrato, but close enough that casual listeners won't know.

**What it's not good at:** Organic instrument fidelity. Suno's audio has a slightly processed, compressed character, great for pop and EDM, wrong for acoustic jazz or orchestral music where dynamics matter. A cello on Suno sounds like a synth pad approximation of a cello. On [Udio](/reviews/udio), it has bow pressure and room resonance. If you're producing acoustic or classical music, Udio's 48kHz output is audibly better.

**Pricing:** Free (50 credits/day, ~10 songs, watermarked), Pro $10/month (2,500 credits, no watermark, WAV exports), Premier $30/month (10,000 credits, voice cloning, custom models).

**Who should use it:** Content creators who need original background music with vocals, songwriters prototyping ideas at speed, and anyone who wants an all-in-one tool, generation, stem separation, and light editing, without leaving the browser.

## 2. Udio, Best for Audio Quality

[Read full Udio review →](/reviews/udio)

**What it's good at:** Sound quality that audio engineers take seriously. 48kHz stereo output with meaningful improvements in dynamic range, instrument separation, and high-frequency detail compared to Suno's 44.1kHz. A jazz trumpet on Udio has breath and valve noise. A classical string section has bow texture. True waveform inpainting is the workflow differentiator, select a 2-second region of the waveform, describe what should change, and Udio replaces just that region. The transition is clean: no clicks, no phase issues, no audible seams. In our test, we inpainted a vocal phrase with awkward pitch correction and the replacement blended perfectly. Style transfer lets you upload a reference track and match its timbral character, the room ambience, the frequency balance, the instrument texture.

**What it's not good at:** Really using the output. Downloads have been disabled since late 2025 during a licensing transition. Udio settled with Universal, Warner, and Merlin and is rebuilding on licensed training data, the cleanest legal posture in AI music, but it means you can generate beautiful audio you can't export. Also slower: Udio works in 30-second increments, so a 3-minute song takes 4-6 generations plus arrangement time (5-10 minutes vs Suno's 30 seconds).

**Pricing:** Free (10 credits/day + 100/month bonus), Standard $10/month (1,200 credits, commercial use, inpainting).

**Who should use it:** Audiophiles working in organic genres, jazz, classical, acoustic, ambient, where Suno's processed character sounds wrong. Producers who want surgical section editing with inpainting. Anyone tracking the AI music space who wants to be ready when Udio's licensed platform launches and downloads return.

## 3. Stable Audio, Best for Royalty-Free Production Assets

[Read full Stable Audio review →](/reviews/stable-audio)

**What it's good at:** Production assets, not finished songs. Stable Audio generates royalty-free loops, drum patterns, bass lines, melodic textures, sound effects, and stems, exportable as WAV with full commercial rights on all tiers. In our beat sampling test, we generated 30 drum loops, 15 bass lines, and 20 melodic textures in under an hour. The variety was strong enough to build 10 distinct hip-hop tracks without any two sounding alike. The stem separation exports individual instrument tracks cleanly, drums, bass, melody, FX, ready to drop into a DAW timeline. This is the tool for producers who build tracks from components, not for people who want a finished song from a text prompt.

**What it's not good at:** Full song generation. Stable Audio doesn't do vocals. It doesn't arrange songs. It doesn't generate a verse-chorus-verse structure. If you type "write me a pop song about summer," Stable Audio generates a 2-minute instrumental loop, not a song. For finished tracks with vocals, use [Suno](/reviews/suno). For audiophile instrumentals, use [Udio](/reviews/udio). Stable Audio is a production tool, not a songwriting tool.

**Pricing:** Free tier (limited generations), paid plans for higher limits and commercial use. The exact pricing depends on the current Stability AI offering.

**Who should use it:** Producers and beat makers who need building blocks, loops, stems, textures, and one-shots, with clean commercial licensing. Content creators who need background music for YouTube, podcasts, or social video without worrying about copyright claims. Sound designers who want AI-generated source material to layer and process in a DAW.

## 4. AIVA, Best for Orchestral and Cinematic Scoring

[Read full AIVA review →](/reviews/aiva)

**What it's good at:** Orchestral composition with full copyright ownership. AIVA is trained on classical and cinematic music, it thinks in terms of orchestral arrangements, not pop song structures. In our test, we asked for a "3-minute cinematic trailer piece, epic brass and strings, building intensity" and got a composition with proper orchestration: French horns doubling the melody, pizzicato strings in the breakdown, timpani rolls building to the climax. The MIDI export is the killer feature for composers, load AIVA's output into Logic Pro, Ableton, or Cubase and edit every single note, adjust velocities, swap instruments, re-arrange sections. On the Pro plan (€49/month), you own 100% of the copyright. No attribution required. No licensing ambiguity. No legal cloud.

**What it's not good at:** Pop songs, beats, or anything with vocals. AIVA has no vocal generation, zero. If you need a hip-hop beat, a pop chorus, or a podcast intro with singing, AIVA is the wrong tool. Its strength is orchestral and classical; its weakness is everything Suno and Udio do well. The free tier also requires AIVA attribution (credit "Composed by AIVA" in your project), which is a dealbreaker for professional commercial work.

**Pricing:** Free (3 downloads/month, AIVA attribution required), Standard €15/month (15 downloads, attribution required), Pro €49/month (300 downloads, 100% copyright, no attribution, MIDI/SFX export).

**Who should use it:** Film composers, game audio designers, and ad agencies who need orchestral scores with clear copyright ownership. Musicians who want AI-generated compositions as starting points for DAW editing via MIDI. Anyone who needs cinematic music and can't tolerate the legal uncertainty surrounding [Suno](/reviews/suno) and [Udio](/reviews/udio)'s training data lawsuits.

## The Legal Situation

The AI music industry has a legal cloud hanging over it. Suno settled with Warner Music (November 2025) but is still fighting Sony and Universal, a key court hearing is scheduled for July 2026. Udio took a different path: settled with Universal, Warner, and Merlin, then disabled downloads to rebuild on licensed training data. AIVA uses its own compositions and public-domain classical works for training, giving it the cleanest legal posture of all four tools.

What this means for you: if you're commercially releasing AI-generated music, the legal risk varies by tool. [AIVA](/reviews/aiva) (Pro) gives you full copyright with no ambiguity. [Suno](/reviews/suno) and [Udio](/reviews/udio) grant commercial rights through their terms of service, but the underlying training data lawsuits could change the rules. [Stable Audio](/reviews/stable-audio) uses Stability AI's licensed dataset, cleaner than Suno/Udio but not as transparent as AIVA's approach. If legal certainty is non-negotiable, AIVA Pro is the safest bet.

## What About ChatGPT for Music?

ChatGPT can't generate audio. It can write lyrics, describe chord progressions, and suggest song structures, but it can't produce sound. If you need actual music, a track you can put in a video, play at an event, or release on Spotify, you need a dedicated AI music tool. ChatGPT is useful as a pre-production assistant: write your lyrics in ChatGPT, then paste them into Suno's Custom mode for generation. But the music generation itself requires specialized tools.

## FAQ

**Can I use AI-generated music on YouTube without copyright issues?** On paid plans, yes. Suno Pro and Premier include commercial usage rights. Stable Audio's paid tiers include full commercial licensing. AIVA Pro gives you 100% copyright. The risk isn't the license from the tool, it's the ongoing lawsuits about training data. As of mid-2026, no court has ruled that AI-generated music trained on copyrighted recordings infringes, but the Sony/Universal vs Suno hearing in July 2026 could change that.

**Which tool produces the most realistic audio?** [Udio](/reviews/udio) ,  48kHz output with better instrument separation, dynamic range, and high-frequency detail than any competitor. The difference is clearest in acoustic and organic genres. For electronic and heavily produced genres, the gap between Udio and [Suno](/reviews/suno) is smaller.

**Can I copyright AI-generated music?** It depends on the tool and your jurisdiction. In the US, purely AI-generated output lacks the "meaningful human authorship" required for copyright. On [AIVA](/reviews/aiva) Pro, you own 100% of the copyright per their terms. For Suno and Udio output, you can claim copyright over substantial human modifications, re-recording vocals, adding live instruments, restructuring the arrangement. The raw AI output alone is unlikely to qualify.

**Which tool is best for background music in videos?** [Suno](/reviews/suno) if you want complete tracks with a specific vibe, type a description, get a 3-minute song. [Stable Audio](/reviews/stable-audio) if you want loops and stems you can arrange yourself. For a YouTube content pipeline producing 20+ videos per month, Stable Audio's component-based approach gives more variety per generation.

**Do these tools work for non-English lyrics?** Suno and Udio support multiple languages for vocal generation, but English produces the most natural-sounding vocals. Less common languages may have pronunciation artifacts. AIVA and Stable Audio are instrumental-focused and don't generate lyrics.

**Where can I hear community-made examples?** AI music creators share full tracks and workflow breakdowns on [r/AIMusic](https://www.reddit.com/r/AIMusic/) and [r/SunoAI](https://www.reddit.com/r/SunoAI/).
