L&D teams have a video problem. Training content needs to be produced, updated, and distributed across departments in multiple languages. Traditional video production costs $1,000-5,000 per video and takes weeks. AI avatar tools reduce this to minutes and dollars.

[Synthesia](/reviews/synthesia) and [HeyGen](/reviews/heygen) are the two market leaders. They solve the same problem from opposite directions. Synthesia prioritizes enterprise readiness: SOC 2, SCORM export, 120+ languages, custom avatars. HeyGen prioritizes speed and flexibility: faster generation, lower cost, webcam-based custom avatars.

I tested both through the lens of an L&D team producing monthly compliance training, onboarding videos, and product walkthroughs.

## Enterprise Readiness: Synthesia Wins

Synthesia is the only AI avatar platform with SOC 2 Type II certification. This matters for any company with compliance requirements around data handling, especially in finance, healthcare, and legal industries.

**Synthesia's enterprise strengths:**
- SOC 2 Type II certified, GDPR compliant
- SCORM-compliant export for LMS integration (Moodle, Cornerstone, SAP Litmos)
- Custom avatar creation with professional studio capture, signed consent forms, and full legal documentation
- Admin controls: user permissions, content approval workflows, usage analytics
- 120+ languages with native-quality pronunciation

**HeyGen's limitations here:**
- SOC 2 certification is not yet complete as of mid-2026
- SCORM export is available but less mature than Synthesia's
- Custom avatars are webcam-based rather than studio-captured — quality is lower, but creation is faster and cheaper
- Admin controls are less granular

For a compliance-heavy enterprise with strict data handling requirements, Synthesia is the safer choice. For a smaller or more agile L&D team where compliance is less of a gate, HeyGen's practical advantages may outweigh the certification gap.

## Speed and Workflow: HeyGen Wins

HeyGen generates videos faster. A 5-minute training video takes approximately 10 minutes from script paste to final download on HeyGen, versus 15-20 minutes on Synthesia. For an L&D team producing 10+ videos per month, this compounds.

**HeyGen's workflow advantages:**
- Multi-scene editor: combine avatar, slides, screen recordings, and text overlays in one video. Synthesia supports slides but the editor is less flexible.
- Custom avatars from webcam recording: no studio session, no travel, no legal review for talent contracts. Record a team member for 2 minutes, and their avatar is ready the same day.
- API access for programmatic generation: if your L&D team has engineering support, you can automate training video production from content updates.

**Synthesia's workflow advantages:**
- Built-in quiz and assessment features: add knowledge checks to training videos without a separate authoring tool
- One-click localization: write your script in English, render in 120+ languages with a single click
- Better collaboration tools for multi-person L&D teams

For teams that value production speed and the ability to use subject-matter experts as avatars with no studio requirements, HeyGen has a clear edge.

## Custom Avatars: Different Approaches

This is where the platforms diverge most sharply.

**Synthesia custom avatars:**
- Professional studio capture (the company sends a team or you visit a partner studio)
- Signed talent release forms, full legal documentation
- Highest quality — studio lighting, professional audio, consistent output
- Cost: approximately $1,000+ per avatar (one-time fee)
- Timeline: weeks

**HeyGen custom avatars:**
- Webcam capture — any team member can record in their home office
- Consent handled through the platform interface
- Quality is good but not professional-studio level
- Cost: included in Enterprise plan (no per-avatar fee)
- Timeline: same day

For a CEO avatar used in company-wide announcements, Synthesia's studio quality justifies the cost. For onboarding videos featuring the HR manager who updates the content every quarter, HeyGen's faster, cheaper approach makes more practical sense.

## My Recommendation

**Choose Synthesia if:**
- Your company requires SOC 2 compliance from vendors
- You need SCORM export for LMS integration
- You produce training in 20+ languages
- You need CEO or executive avatars with studio-quality output
- Legal and procurement teams need to review vendor security documentation

**Choose HeyGen if:**
- Speed and iteration matter more than certifications
- You want to create avatars of team members without studio sessions
- Your L&D team has a limited budget
- You produce videos in fewer than 40 languages
- You want API access for automated video generation

## FAQ

**Can I use both platforms together?**
Yes. Some companies use Synthesia for compliance training and executive communications, and HeyGen for internal onboarding and quick-turnaround content. The combined cost is still a fraction of traditional video production.

**Which produces more natural-looking avatars?**
Synthesia's studio-captured avatars look more natural, especially in the lips and eye movement. HeyGen's webcam avatars are good but have a slight uncanniness on close inspection. For training videos viewed on laptop screens at normal distance, most employees will not notice the difference.

**How do these compare to live-action training videos?**
AI avatar videos have lower production cost and faster updates but lower engagement than a well-produced live-action video with a charismatic presenter. For compliance and procedural training (where engagement is secondary to completion), AI avatars are more than sufficient. For leadership and culture content, live video still performs better.
