Turn a product photo into a five-second ad. Make a character illustration move without rotoscoping. Animate a still frame from last year's shoot instead of booking a new one. Image-to-video is the most practical AI video workflow for production work, because you already know what the first frame looks like. Text-to-video rolls the dice on every generation. Image-to-video keeps your composition and your product, then adds motion. That preference shows up in the data too: an [analysis of 108 million AI video generations by Magnific](https://www.magnific.com/blog/image-to-video/), tracked from late 2025 to August 2026, found that 90.7% of them started from an image rather than a text prompt, an 18-to-1 ratio.

We ran the same batch of stills through the three tools creators ask about most: [Kling AI](https://klingai.com/), [Runway Gen-3](https://runwayml.com/), and [Luma Dream Machine](https://lumalabs.ai/dream-machine). Each animates images differently, and each has a different idea of what "control" means. The short version: Kling AI is the best pick for most still-image work, Runway Gen-3 wins when clips need professional post-production, and Luma Dream Machine takes it when you need exact control over how a shot starts and ends.

## Kling AI: Best Motion Realism Per Dollar

![Kling AI logo, the budget pick for image-to-video motion](/logos/kling-ai.png)

[Kling AI](/reviews/kling-ai) treats image-to-video as its main event. Upload a still and you get three ways to drive the motion: a text prompt for the action, a video reference that copies the movement style from existing footage, and an audio reference that syncs generation to sound. For product work, the Subject Consistency 3.0 feature matters most. You upload reference images and Kling locks a product's or character's appearance across multiple generated clips, which is the difference between a one-off animation and a usable campaign set.

The motion quality is where Kling pulls ahead on price. Physics reads correctly: fabric drapes, liquid pours, hands grip objects without the finger-melting artifacts common in cheaper generators. Native 4K output arrives at lower pricing tiers than either competitor, which counts if your ad platform specs call for high-resolution masters. Kuaishou Technology built Kling to compete on raw generation quality, and still-image animation is where that shows.

The friction shows up in credits. Kling's free tier gives you 66 credits per day, enough to test the workflow, but a single 5-second 4K generation can eat a large share of a paid plan's monthly allowance, and failed generations still deduct credits. Budget an extra 20-30% on top of your expected usage when comparing plans. For volume production, do your prompt testing at 720p and reserve 4K generations for final output. For the full model breakdown, see our [Kling AI review](/reviews/kling-ai).

## Runway Gen-3: Best for the Post-Production Pipeline

![Runway Gen-3 logo](/logos/runway-gen-3.png)

[Runway Gen-3](/reviews/runway-gen-3) is the only tool in this comparison that is a complete studio, not a generator with export options. Image-to-video sits alongside motion control, style transfer, and a professional editing suite with masking, rotoscoping, and motion tracking in the same workspace. That changes the workflow math: with Kling or Luma, a generation is the end of the AI step and the start of an NLE session. With Runway, you generate, then fix the details in the same browser tab.

For commercial work, that integration is worth real money. A generated clip that has a flickering logo, a warped edge, or an unwanted background element is a re-roll on Kling or Luma. On Runway, you mask the problem area and patch it. Clients who expect revision rounds will feel the difference immediately, and the cinematic quality ceiling remains the highest in this group.

Two costs stand out. The $15/month Standard plan looks entry-level until you count credits: it covers roughly 12-15 Gen-3 clips per month, so anyone creating regularly lands on the $28-35/month Pro tier. And the default data policy gives Runway a license to train on your inputs and outputs on every plan below Enterprise. If your client contracts include data restrictions, that clause matters more than the subscription price. See our [Runway Gen-3 review](/reviews/runway-gen-3) for the full plan table.

## Luma Dream Machine: Best for Camera Control and Keyframing

![Luma Dream Machine logo](/logos/luma-dream-machine.svg)

[Luma Dream Machine](/reviews/luma-dream-machine) has one feature that neither competitor matches at this price point: keyframing. You set the start frame and the end frame, and Luma generates the transition between them. For product videos, that means showing a box opening, a lid closing, or a label rotating from front to back, all with exact control over where the clip begins and ends. Instead of prompting "camera pans around the bottle" and hoping, you define the two states and let the model connect them.

Image-to-video starts at roughly 12 credits per generation, and the pace is the fastest of the three for concept iteration. Character Lock, available from the Pro tier, uses CLIP embeddings and on-the-fly LoRA adapters to keep a character consistent across multiple clips, similar to what Runway Gen-4 offers at a higher price.

The ceiling is 1080p, with CinemaScope arriving on Pro plans. If your deliverables need 4K masters, Luma is the wrong starting point, and Kling is $6.99/month against Luma's $9.99 for the privilege. Character Lock sits behind the Pro plan at $94.99/month, which prices consistency out of reach for small teams. Luma is the tool for storyboard-speed iteration and camera-accurate transitions, not for maximum resolution. Full plan details are in our [Luma Dream Machine review](/reviews/luma-dream-machine).

## FAQ

**Can I do image-to-video for free?** All three tools have free tiers with different limits. Kling gives 66 credits per day, the most generous for testing an ongoing workflow. Runway gives 125 one-time credits, and Luma about 30 generations per month. Free-tier output is capped at 720p with watermarks on all three, so treat free tiers as evaluation tools, not production options.

**What is the difference between image-to-video and text-to-video?** Text-to-video generates everything from a prompt, including composition, which is why results vary run to run. Image-to-video takes a still you supply and animates it, so the composition stays fixed and only the motion is generated. For product work, ads, and brand content, image-to-video gives you the control that text-to-video cannot.

**How long can AI image-to-video clips be?** Most generations run 5 seconds, with longer options on higher tiers. Luma offers 10-second clips on its Pro plan, and Kling supports extended durations on paid plans. Real workflows chain multiple generations and cut them together, rather than generating one long take.

**Can I use AI-generated videos commercially?** It depends on the plan, not the tool. Luma grants commercial rights from the Standard tier ($29.99/month). Kling's paid plans include commercial use. Runway grants usage rights across plans but trains on your data below Enterprise, so check the terms your client contracts require before settling on a tier.

**Which tool is best for product videos?** Kling for 4K output on a budget, Runway if your video needs retouching and client revisions, Luma if the shot depends on a precise before-and-after transition. Test one product photo through all three free tiers before committing, since motion quality varies by subject type.
