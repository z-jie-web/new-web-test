Companies used to spend $50,000 on a single training video shoot. Now they produce entire course libraries without a camera. Marketing teams localize product demos into 30 languages without hiring 30 voice actors. Sales teams send personalized video messages at scale without pressing record.

The category moved again in June 2026, so I re-checked the official pricing pages before refreshing this guide. The core decision is still the same: all of these tools put a realistic talking human on screen without filming one, but they optimize for different workflows. Pick the wrong one and you are paying team-level pricing for features you will never use.

## The 30-Second Verdict

- **Running corporate training, compliance, or L&D at scale?** [Synthesia](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing). SOC 2 Type II, SSO, team workspaces, and SCORM on Enterprise. Still the safest enterprise pick.
- **Creating marketing content or localizing into multiple languages?** [HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/pricing). Better expressiveness, strong translation workflow, and broader creator-friendly pricing than Synthesia.
- **Want personalized video outreach or talking photos?** [D-ID](https://www.d-id.com/pricing/studio/). Upload a headshot, get a talking presenter. Best fit when personalization matters more than polished stock avatars.
- **Building interactive training from decks and documents?** [Elai](https://elai.io/pricing). Slide-to-video from PPTX, PDF, or URL, plus quizzes, branching, and Panopto-friendly workflow.

## Synthesia: The Enterprise Lock-In

Synthesia still owns the corporate training conversation, but the packaging changed. The public pricing page now starts with a free Basic tier, then jumps to Starter at $29/month and Creator at $89/month, with Enterprise carrying the bigger governance features. It also lists 160+ languages and voices, 125+ avatars on Starter, 180+ on Creator, and 240+ on Enterprise. If you work at a company with a compliance department, those details matter more than marketing polish.

The avatars look like corporate trainers. Polished. Professional. A bit stiff. That restraint is intentional, when your audience is employees watching mandatory training, you do not want an avatar that feels like a TikTok filter.

The downsides are still real. Entry pricing is higher than the old version of this article suggested, and the best governance features remain locked behind Enterprise. Avatar customization is also more restrained than HeyGen's. For L&D content, that restraint is a feature. For marketing content that needs personality, HeyGen fits better.

## HeyGen: The Expressive One

HeyGen's avatars still feel more expressive than Synthesia's. Lip movements sync better across translated videos, and the product now has a clearer ladder: Free, Creator at $29/month, Pro at $49/month, and Business at $149/month plus $20 per extra seat.

The localization features are where HeyGen pulls ahead: record in English, produce versions in dozens of languages, and the avatar adapts to each language's phonemes. The official pricing page now lists 175+ languages and dialects on paid plans — the kind of detail global marketing teams check before buying.

API access is available for developers integrating avatar video into custom workflows. Limited free trial available.

## D-ID: The Photo Animator

D-ID does one thing the others skip: upload a still photo and get a talking head without building a full avatar workflow. No studio recording. No long avatar onboarding. Just a headshot and a script.

This enables use cases Synthesia and HeyGen cannot address: personalized sales outreach with the actual salesperson's face, educational content with historical figures, creative marketing with custom characters that do not exist in anyone's avatar library.

D-ID's public Studio pricing page is less transparent than Synthesia's or HeyGen's, but the FAQ still confirms a trial path, minute-based usage, and watermarks on Trial and Lite plans. That alone tells you the product is still optimized around lightweight personalization and experimentation rather than cleanly tiered enterprise packaging.

The avatars are less polished than Synthesia's corporate presenters or HeyGen's expressive performers. But that is not the point. The use case is personalized outreach, AI spokespeople, or talking-photo workflows, not standardized corporate training.

## Elai: The Training Specialist

Elai was acquired by Panopto in 2024, and that still tells you everything about its focus. It is not chasing general-purpose creator video. It solves one problem well: turning existing training materials into structured, trackable video courses that live inside an LMS.

The core workflow starts with content you already have: a PowerPoint deck, a PDF handbook, a compliance document, a URL. Elai parses each slide or section into a video scene, assigns an AI avatar to present it, and generates the video with synced lip movements and voiceover. You can embed quizzes between scenes, build branching paths where learner choices determine the next scene, and add clickable hotspots. The output exports as SCORM 1.2 or SCORM 2004 packages, zip files you upload to Moodle, Thinkific, or any SCORM-compliant LMS that track learner progress, quiz scores, and completion status.

This is different from exporting an MP4 and hoping someone watches it. For compliance training where proof of completion is mandatory, Elai's interactive features are not add-ons, they are the reason to choose it.

The trade-offs are clearer on the current pricing page. Creator is $29 monthly or $23 on annual billing for 15 minutes a month. Team is $125 monthly or $100 annually for 50 minutes, 3 editors, custom images and fonts, Ultra 4K video, and a bundled selfie avatar plus voice clone on annual Team. Enterprise adds SSO, Brand Kit, workspaces, and premium support. The plan matrix also shows SCORM export in the higher-end collaboration layer, so if SCORM is a hard requirement, confirm the exact current tier before buying.

Avatar realism still trails HeyGen and Synthesia. Elai's presenters look professional and clean, but clearly AI-generated, which is fine for internal training where the content carries the weight.

Elai integrates natively with Panopto's video management ecosystem. For organizations already using Panopto, Elai is the obvious content creation layer. For everyone else, [Colossyan](/reviews/colossyan) is worth comparing directly, it includes SCORM export on all paid plans, not just Enterprise.

## FAQ

**Best for corporate training?** Synthesia is still the safest default for regulated industries. Elai becomes more attractive when your starting point is slide decks, PDFs, and LMS packaging rather than avatar polish.

**Can I create an avatar of myself?** Yes, but the route differs. Synthesia and HeyGen lean toward structured avatar creation, while D-ID remains the fastest path if you want a talking-photo style presenter from minimal source material.

**Which supports the most languages?** Synthesia lists 160+ languages and voices on its public pricing page; HeyGen shows 175+ languages and dialects on paid plans. For expressive localization across those languages, HeyGen produces more natural lip-sync.

**How much should I budget?** The solo entry point is now more like $29/month if you want serious work in Synthesia or HeyGen. Team pricing climbs quickly after that, especially once you need collaboration, dubbing, custom avatars, or API access.

**Which platform supports SCORM export for LMS?** Elai, Colossyan, and Synthesia all sit in this conversation, but the exact tiering changed enough that you should verify the current plan matrix before buying. Synthesia clearly lists SCORM on Enterprise, while Elai positions it in the higher-end collaboration layer rather than the entry plan.

For L&D teams building internal training programs, see our [how to use AI avatars for corporate training videos](/blog/how-to-use-ai-avatars-for-corporate-training) guide.

**What should I do right now?** Start a free trial on the platform that matches your primary use case. Make one video for a real project, not a test, a real deliverable. If the output quality and workflow speed justify the subscription, commit. If not, try the next platform.
