## FaceFusion: The Power Tool for Full Control

![FaceFusion logo, open-source face swap for deepfake videos](/logos/facefusion.png)

[FaceFusion](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion) is the tool the other tools get compared against, and the 3.6.1 release in May 2026 stretched the lead. The HyperSwap 256px models deliver four times the pixel resolution of the older inswapper models, picking up eye reflections and skin texture that paid cloud tools struggle to match. It runs locally, which means no upload caps, no watermarks, and no third party holding your source footage.

Control is where it separates from every cloud option. Pixel Boost sharpens the blend between the swapped face and the original body. Live Portrait transfers facial expressions from a driving video onto the target, which turns a static swap into a directed performance. Wav2Lip integration syncs lip movement to an audio track, which is the feature that turns a static swap into a talking deepfake. Multi-face mode handles group shots in a single pass.

The cost is the setup. FaceFusion wants Python 3.10+, a CUDA-compatible GPU (an RTX 3060 or better is the practical floor), and comfort with a command line, though an official GUI and web UI mode now soften that. Budget 30-60 minutes for first-time installation and a model download of roughly 2GB. For developers, privacy-conscious creators, and anyone doing this at volume, that one-time friction buys unlimited use forever. Read our [FaceFusion review](/reviews/facefusion) for the full setup walkthrough.

## FaceMagic: Best for Short-Form Video Creators

![FaceMagic logo, mobile face swap app for short-form video](/logos/facemagic.png)

[FaceMagic](https://facemagic.ai) takes the opposite approach: everything happens on your phone, and you upload your own footage rather than getting locked into a template library like Reface. For TikTok and Reels-length clips, the workflow is fast enough to iterate in an afternoon. For a side-by-side on those two approaches, see our [FaceMagic vs Reface comparison](/compare/facemagic-vs-reface).

The honest caveat is quality. Output is mid-tier: visibly better than free browser tools, clearly behind desktop software. Results track input conditions closely. Matching light direction, similar face angles, and high-resolution source material produce clean swaps; mismatched lighting, extreme angles, or fast motion produce flickering and tracking drift. The free tier caps you at 3-5 watermarked swaps per day, and the unlimited subscription sits behind an in-app purchase with pricing that the company does not publish clearly. Test the free tier with your actual footage before paying.

## Swapface: Best for Real-Time and Live Streaming

![Swapface logo, real-time face swap for live streams](/logos/swapface.png)

[Swapface](https://swapface.org) does one thing the others cannot: live face replacement on a webcam feed. It plugs into OBS, so streamers can wear a different face for an entire broadcast, and it works on video calls for the prank-and-entertainment use case. The [FaceFusion vs Swapface comparison](/compare/facefusion-vs-swapface) covers the two live-capable options side by side.

Two constraints shape the experience. It is Windows-only, and real-time swapping needs a reasonable GPU, since every frame is processed as it arrives. Latency and quality scale with your hardware, so mid-range laptops will show artifacts that a desktop with a modern card will not. If your content is recorded rather than live, FaceFusion gives you better output for zero ongoing cost; Swapface earns its subscription in the live niche.

## DeepSwapper: The Free Quick-Test Option

![DeepSwapper logo, free browser face swap tool](/logos/deepswapper.svg)

[DeepSwapper](/reviews/deepswapper) is a browser tool that works with no signup and no cost, which makes it the fastest way to see how a face swap will look before committing to a heavier tool. The limit is fundamental: photos only, no video. For deepfake videos it works as a previsualization step, letting you test face pairings and angles on stills before running the full pipeline elsewhere. Batch processing is missing too, so it stays a one-off tool rather than a workflow. See our [DeepSwapper vs FaceMagic comparison](/compare/deepswapper-vs-facemagic) for the free-versus-freemium breakdown.

Two more tools round out the six we track. [Reface](/reviews/reface) is the mainstream consumer app, with a template library built for viral clips rather than custom footage. [Remaker Face Swap](/reviews/remaker-face-swap) bundles face swap into a broader image toolkit with background removal and image cleanup, better for photo editing than video work.

## The Legal and Ethical Baseline in 2026

This is the part most tool roundups skip, and in 2026 it is the part with the largest consequences.

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules took effect on August 2, 2026. They require deepfakes to be disclosed with a clear, perceivable label at first exposure, with penalties reaching €15 million or 3% of global turnover. Narrow exemptions exist for evidently artistic or satirical works, and commercial advertising does not qualify for them. The same month, California's AI Transparency Act began requiring machine-readable watermarks on AI-generated content, and New York's synthetic performer law started requiring advertisers to disclose AI-generated faces, voices, or digital doubles in ads, with penalties starting at $1,000 per violation. The US federal TAKE IT DOWN Act criminalizes non-consensual intimate imagery including AI-generated deepfakes, and platforms must remove reported content within 48 hours. Election deepfake rules exist in 31 states, according to [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/), which tracks that legislation, with disclosure requirements for political content.

The patterns worth internalizing: consent decides legality far more than the technique, disclosure is becoming mandatory in most jurisdictions, and commercial content gets less legal shelter than satire or art. [Reality Defender maintains a state-of-deepfake-regulation summary](https://www.realitydefender.com/insights/the-state-of-deepfake-regulations) if you want to check your own jurisdiction. ## FAQ

**What is the best face swap tool for deepfake videos?** FaceFusion for quality and control, since it runs locally with no usage limits and the 3.6.1 HyperSwap models beat cloud tools on resolution. FaceMagic is the practical pick if you work from a phone, and Swapface leads if the video is live.

**Are deepfake face swap videos legal?** It depends on consent, labeling, and jurisdiction rather than the tool. Non-consensual intimate imagery is criminal under the federal TAKE IT DOWN Act, the EU requires deepfake disclosure labels, and 31 US states regulate election deepfakes. Satire and art get more leeway than commercial content in most frameworks.

**Can I make a deepfake video for free?** FaceFusion is fully free and open source if you have a compatible GPU. FaceMagic gives 3-5 watermarked swaps daily, and DeepSwapper is free for photo swaps. Video without watermarks or caps means FaceFusion or a paid tier.

**Do I need a GPU for face swap videos?** For FaceFusion and Swapface, yes. FaceFusion recommends an RTX 3060 or better for reasonable speeds, and Swapface needs one for real-time streaming. Mobile and browser tools handle the processing server-side, which is why their output quality and daily limits are lower.

**Which tool makes the most realistic deepfakes?** FaceFusion, particularly for face detail and expression control with Live Portrait and Wav2Lip. Realism also depends on input quality: matching lighting, similar angles, and high-resolution footage matter more than any single setting.

**Do I have to label a face swap video as AI?** In the EU, yes for content covered by Article 50, with narrow artistic exemptions. California requires machine-readable marking, New York requires disclosure for synthetic performers in ads, and most large platforms have their own AI content labeling rules. When in doubt, label it.
