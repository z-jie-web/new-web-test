Background music is one of the hardest parts of YouTube production. You need it for every video. You cannot use copyrighted tracks. Royalty-free libraries cost $15-30/month. And the free options sound like elevator muzak from 2008.

AI music generators changed this equation. You describe what you want — "upbeat electronic track, 120 BPM, positive energy" — and the AI produces a unique, royalty-free track in under a minute. Some of them even include vocals.

I tested four AI music generators specifically for YouTube creator workflows: [Suno](/reviews/suno), [Soundraw](/reviews/soundraw), [Beatoven](/reviews/beatoven), and [Mubert](/reviews/mubert). Here is which ones produce music good enough to put in your actual videos.

## The quick verdict

- **Best for full songs with vocals:** [Suno](/reviews/suno) — generates complete songs with lyrics, melody, and vocals from a text prompt
- **Best for customizable background tracks:** [Soundraw](/reviews/soundraw) — the most control over structure, mood, and arrangement
- **Best for mood-based scoring:** [Beatoven](/reviews/beatoven) — generates music based on emotional mood, not genre
- **Best for API-driven content:** [Mubert](/reviews/mubert) — real-time music generation through API for dynamic content

## Suno: Full songs with vocals

Suno is the AI music generator that broke through to mainstream attention. It generates complete songs — vocals, melody, instruments, lyrics — from a single text prompt. Type "uplifting pop song about chasing your dreams" and Suno produces a 2-4 minute track with actual sung vocals.

**What works for YouTube:**
- Vocal quality is the best in class. If your content needs a theme song, intro jingle, or original music for a music video, Suno produces something usable in one generation.
- The free tier gives you 50 credits per day (about 10 songs). For a creator who publishes weekly, this may be enough without paying.
- Lyric generation is surprisingly good. You can provide your own lyrics or let Suno write them based on your theme.
- Multiple versions are generated per prompt. You get 2 variations and can extend, remix, or adjust.

**What does not work:**
- The tracks sound like AI-generated pop music. If your channel needs subtle background music for talking-head videos, Suno is overkill. It is better for standalone songs than background scoring.
- Copyright status is still evolving. Suno-generated music is yours to use, but the legal landscape around AI-generated music is not fully settled. For commercial YouTube channels with sponsorships, this is worth monitoring.
- No mood sliders or structure controls. You describe what you want in natural language and get what Suno decides to give you. For precise background music, Soundraw or Beatoven give more control.

For the full evaluation, read our [Suno review](/reviews/suno).

## Soundraw: The most customizable

Soundraw takes the opposite approach from Suno. Instead of generating full songs from a prompt, it gives you a toolkit: choose a genre, set a mood, pick a length, select instruments, and adjust the energy curve throughout the track.

**What works for YouTube:**
- The arrangement controls are unmatched. You can specify that the first 30 seconds should be calm, then build energy for the main section, then fade out. This maps directly to YouTube video structure: intro → main content → outro.
- Every track is customizable after generation. Change the tempo, add or remove instruments, adjust the mood. You are not locked into what the AI produced on the first pass.
- Royalty-free licensing is clear and perpetual. Once you download a track, you own the license forever, even if you cancel your subscription.
- The Creator plan at $16.99/month includes unlimited downloads, which is the best value for high-volume YouTube production.

**What does not work:**
- No vocals. Soundraw generates instrumental music only. If you need a theme song with singing, you will need Suno.
- The free tier only lets you generate 3 tracks per day and does not include downloads. You need the paid plan for actual YouTube use.
- Genre options are limited compared to dedicated music production tools. You get pop, electronic, cinematic, ambient, and a few others — not niche genres.

For detailed features and pricing, see our [Soundraw review](/reviews/soundraw) and comparison with [Beatoven](/compare/soundraw-vs-beatoven).

## Beatoven: Mood-based scoring

Beatoven approaches AI music generation through the lens of emotion. Instead of genres and instruments, you describe the mood you want: "tense," "hopeful," "melancholic," "triumphant." The AI generates music that matches that emotional arc.

**What works for YouTube:**
- The mood-to-music pipeline is perfect for documentary, storytelling, and essay-style content. If you are making a video about a serious topic and need music that matches the emotional weight, Beatoven nails the tone.
- Royalty-free licensing is the cleanest in the market. Beatoven trained its models on AudioSparx-licensed music, so the copyright status is unambiguous. For commercial YouTube channels, this is the lowest-risk option.
- The free tier includes 15 minutes of generated music per month. For a creator who publishes 1-2 videos per week, this may be enough on the free plan.
- Timeline-based editing lets you map music to your video's emotional arc. Tension during the conflict, relief during the resolution, calm during the conclusion.

**What does not work:**
- The catalog of generated styles is smaller than Soundraw. You get less variety in instrumentation and arrangement.
- No vocal generation. Like Soundraw, Beatoven produces instrumental music only.
- The interface is more complex than Suno's one-prompt approach. If you just want "some background music," Beatoven's mood-based system requires more thought upfront.

For the full breakdown, read our [Beatoven review](/reviews/beatoven).

## Mubert: API-driven for dynamic content

Mubert is different from the other three. It generates music in real-time through an API, making it suitable for dynamic content that changes based on context. Think of it as a music engine, not a music generator.

**What works for YouTube:**
- Real-time generation means infinite variation. No two tracks are ever the same, which is useful for channels that publish daily and need fresh music every time.
- The API lets you integrate music generation into automated workflows. If you have a script that produces video drafts, Mubert can generate matching music as part of the pipeline.
- Genre diversity is the broadest of the four tools. Electronic, ambient, classical, jazz, hip-hop beats — Mubert covers more styles than Soundraw or Beatoven.
- The free tier includes 25 tracks per month, which is enough for 4-6 weekly videos.

**What does not work:**
- Track quality is more inconsistent than the other tools. Some generations are excellent, others sound generic. You may need to generate 3-4 tracks to find one that works.
- No mood-based controls like Beatoven. You select genre and duration, and Mubert handles the rest.
- The web interface is functional but not as polished as Soundraw's arrangement editor.

## FAQ

**Can I monetize YouTube videos with AI-generated music?**
Yes. All four tools grant commercial use rights on their paid plans. Suno's terms are the most permissive — you own the generated tracks. Soundraw, Beatoven, and Mubert grant perpetual licenses on paid plans. Always check the current terms before monetizing.

**Will YouTube's Content ID flag AI-generated music?**
As of 2026, no reported cases of AI-generated music triggering Content ID claims from these four tools. However, AI music copyright law is still developing. For maximum safety, use tools with clear licensing (Beatoven's AudioSparx-trained model has the cleanest legal foundation).

**How much does AI music cost for a small YouTube channel?**
Suno's free tier (10 songs/day) is the most generous. Beatoven gives 15 minutes/month free. Mubert offers 25 tracks/month free. Soundraw requires a paid plan ($16.99/month) for downloads. If you are bootstrapping, start with Suno or Beatoven's free tiers.

**Do I still need a royalty-free music subscription?**
For most YouTube creators, AI music generators replace the need for a subscription to Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or similar services. The quality gap has closed enough that AI-generated tracks work for 90% of use cases. Keep a subscription only if you need highly specific, hand-crafted music for premium content.
