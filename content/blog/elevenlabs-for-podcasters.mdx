## The 30-Second Workflow

Here is the production path that works for most solo podcasters and YouTubers:

1. Write your script in plain text — no formatting, no markdown
2. Pick the **Multilingual v2** model (not Turbo — more on this below)
3. Select a voice from the Library or clone your own
4. Paste 500-800 words at a time (the sweet spot for consistent quality)
5. Set Stability to 40-50%, Clarity to 70-80%
6. Generate, listen, regenerate sections that sound off
7. Export as 192kbps MP3 or 48kHz WAV
8. Import into Descript, Audacity, or your DAW for final editing

That is the core loop. Below is why each step matters and where things go wrong.

## Which ElevenLabs Model to Use for Voiceover

ElevenLabs offers five models. For podcasting and YouTube, you only need to care about two:

| Model | Best for | Skip if |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Multilingual v2** | Narration, monologue, storytelling | You only need short-form (< 30 sec) |
| **Turbo v2.5** | Real-time, conversational, low latency | You are producing long-form content |

**Multilingual v2 is the right model for 90% of podcast and YouTube voiceover.** It produces the most expressive, natural-sounding long-form narration. The trade-off is generation speed — it takes 2-3 seconds per sentence — but the output quality is worth it.

Turbo v2.5 generates near-instantly but sounds flatter. Use it for short segments: ad reads, channel intros, CTAs where you want quick turnaround. For a 20-minute episode, Multilingual v2 is the only choice that will not make listeners notice it is AI.

The Flash, English v1, and Multilingual v1 models are legacy. Ignore them unless you have a specific compatibility need.

## Voice Selection: Library vs. Cloning

### Library Voices

Start with the Library. It has hundreds of pre-made voices sorted by age, gender, and accent. For podcasting, filter for "Narration" or "Educational" styles — these are trained on longer-form speech patterns and handle pacing better than "Conversational" or "Character" voices.

Listen to at least 30 seconds of each candidate voice reading your actual script, not ElevenLabs' sample text. A voice that sounds warm reading "The quick brown fox" can sound robotic reading your intro paragraph about SaaS metrics.

### Voice Cloning

Instant Clone needs 60 seconds of clean audio. It is fast and surprisingly good for the input length. The result captures your vocal texture but may lose some expressiveness — it works best for supplementary content, not your main narration.

Professional Clone needs 30 minutes of studio-quality audio. This produces a near-indistinguishable replica with breath patterns, pacing, and emotional range. If you are a solo creator who wants to "narrate" episodes without recording your voice every time, this is the upgrade worth paying for.

One workflow podcasters often miss: record one Professional Clone of yourself, then use it to generate a first-pass narration. Import into Descript, edit the script there, and let it regenerate only the sections you changed. This cuts production time by 60-70% versus recording every take live.

## Settings That Change the Output

### Stability (0-100%)

This controls how much the model varies between generations of the same text. **Lower = more expressive but less consistent.** Higher = more monotone but more predictable.

For podcast narration: **40-50%.** This gives enough variation to sound human without random pitch jumps between sentences.

For ad reads or CTA segments: **60-70%.** You want these to sound controlled and authoritative.

Below 30% creates a "warbling" effect — the voice changes character mid-sentence. It is occasionally useful for creative character work. It is never useful for professional narration.

### Clarity (0-100%)

This controls how precisely the model articulates each phoneme. Higher clarity reduces mumbling and slurring but can make the voice sound clinical.

For podcasting: **70-80%.** You want clear diction without sounding like a GPS navigation voice.

If your script contains technical terms, brand names, or non-English words, bump clarity to 85-90% for those segments. ElevenLabs handles technical vocabulary better than any other TTS engine, but it still needs the clarity headroom to nail unfamiliar terms.

### Style Exaggeration (0-100%)

This amplifies the emotional cues in the text. Available only on Multilingual v2.

For straight narration: **0-20%.** The model already infers natural prosody from punctuation and context. Amplification usually makes it worse.

For dramatic segments or storytelling: **20-40%.** This can add useful emphasis, but preview every segment — what sounds "emotional" in isolation sounds "melodramatic" in context.

## Script Formatting Tips for Better Output

The single biggest factor in voice quality is not the model or the settings. It is how you format your script.

**What works:**
- Short sentences. Break anything over 20 words into two.
- Natural punctuation. Commas create micro-pauses. Periods create full pauses. Ellipses create hesitation.
- Paragraph breaks between ideas. The model uses paragraph context for pacing.
- Numbers and symbols spelled out. "Twenty twenty-six" not "2026." "Five hundred dollars" not "$500."

**What does not work:**
- ALL CAPS. The model may interpret it as shouting or emphasis. It rarely sounds natural.
- Markdown formatting. Strip it. Bold markers, bullet points, and headers confuse the parser.
- Long unbroken paragraphs. ElevenLabs generates one paragraph at a time. A 300-word wall of text will come out as a breathless monologue.

**The 500-word sweet spot:** ElevenLabs generates in paragraph chunks. At 500-800 words per generation, you get consistent voice quality from start to finish. Longer than that and later paragraphs degrade slightly — the model starts to "drift" on pacing and emphasis.

## Export and Integration with Editing Software

| Format | When to use |
|--------|------------|
| MP3 192kbps | Final delivery for podcast hosting (Buzzsprout, Libsyn, Spotify) |
| WAV 48kHz | Import into DAW for mixing with music and effects |
| MP3 128kbps | Quick drafts, client previews |

For Descript users: export as WAV, import into Descript, and use Descript's transcript-based editing to fine-tune timing and remove filler words. Descript will re-transcribe the AI-generated audio, which lets you edit it as if you recorded it yourself. This is the fastest post-production workflow I have found.

For Audacity/Reaper/Logic users: export individual segments as WAV files rather than one long track. This gives you per-segment control in your DAW for leveling, EQ, and pacing adjustments without having to slice a single long file.

## What ElevenLabs Cannot Do (Yet)

- **Multi-speaker conversation from one generation.** If you need a dialogue between two voices, generate each speaker separately and stitch them in your editor.
- **Real-time audio streaming for live shows.** Turbo v2.5 is fast but not sub-second fast. For live AI voice, [Play.ht](/reviews/play-ht) has lower-latency conversational models.
- **Singing or melodic speech.** ElevenLabs is speech-only. For AI singing, see our [best AI music generators](/blog/best-ai-music-generators) guide.
- **Background noise removal.** Audio is generated clean. Any noise comes from your side. Use a noise gate in your DAW if needed.

## FAQ

**Can I use ElevenLabs-generated audio for monetized YouTube videos?**
Yes, on any paid plan (Starter at $5/month and above). The free tier does not include commercial usage rights. Your monetization status on YouTube is unaffected — the license covers the audio itself.

**Which plan do I need as a podcaster?**
Creator plan ($22/month) is the sweet spot. It includes Professional Voice Cloning, 100K characters per month (roughly 2-3 hours of audio), and commercial licensing. Starter ($5/month) works for testing but runs out fast — 30K characters is about one 30-minute episode.

**How long does it take to generate a full podcast episode?**
A 20-minute episode at 500 words per generation takes roughly 15-20 minutes of generation time on Multilingual v2. Add another 20-30 minutes for listening, regenerating sections, and fine-tuning. Total: about 45 minutes from script to final audio.

**Does ElevenLabs support languages other than English?**
Yes, Multilingual v2 supports 29 languages. Quality varies: English, German, French, Spanish, and Portuguese are excellent. Hindi, Arabic, and Korean are good but less consistent. For non-English podcasting, test your specific language with a 2-minute sample before committing.

**Can I use ElevenLabs if I already have a co-host who records live?**
Yes. Generate your narration segments with ElevenLabs, record your co-host live, and mix them in your DAW. The AI voice and human voice will sit naturally together if you match the reverb/EQ settings on both tracks.
