DeepSeek is the Chinese AI lab that shipped an open-weight reasoning model competitive with GPT-4 and Claude, then released the weights for free. If you follow AI benchmarks, you already know the numbers: DeepSeek R1 scores in the top tier on MATH, HumanEval, and GPQA. The 1M token context window is the longest in class. The API costs roughly 1/10th to 1/50th of the frontier competitors. Those three facts explain why DeepSeek went from zero to one of the most discussed AI releases of 2025.

But benchmarks are not the product. The product is what happens when you sit down and use DeepSeek for daily work: the latency from outside Asia, the content restrictions on certain topics, the rough English localization. This review is about the gap between the numbers and the experience.

## Key Features

- **DeepSeek R1 reasoning model** — R1 is the model that put DeepSeek on the map. It uses chain-of-thought reasoning by default, showing its work step by step before giving an answer. On math, formal logic, and competitive programming problems, R1's output quality is in the same tier as GPT-4o and Claude Opus, as confirmed by [independent benchmarks on LiveBench](https://livebench.ai) and the [R1 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). The difference: you can download the weights and run it yourself. For a detailed breakdown of how the reasoning models compare in practice, see our [Best AI Chatbots guide](/blog/best-ai-chatbots).
- **1M token context window** — The longest in class. GPT-4 Turbo offers 128K, Claude offers 200K, Gemini offers up to 1M (with degraded quality at the tail). DeepSeek's 1M window holds up for most of the range. This matters less for casual chat and more for tasks like feeding an entire codebase, a full research paper corpus, or a book-length legal document into one prompt. If you regularly work with large documents, the context window alone is a reason to keep DeepSeek in your toolkit.
- **Open-weight release** — DeepSeek releases model weights under a permissive license. You can download R1, host it on your own infrastructure, fine-tune it on proprietary data, and never send a token to DeepSeek's servers. For enterprises with data residency requirements, compliance constraints, or air-gapped environments, this is the difference between "can use" and "can't use." No other frontier-grade reasoning model offers this.
- **API with dramatically lower pricing** — DeepSeek's API costs $0.14-$0.28 per million input tokens depending on the model, compared to $2.50-$15 for GPT-4 and Claude. For high-volume use cases (customer support automation, batch document processing, large-scale evaluation), the cost difference compounds fast. A team processing 10 million tokens daily saves roughly $25,000 to $150,000 per year compared to GPT-4.
- **Web search integration** — DeepSeek's chat interface includes a search toggle that pulls real-time web results into responses. Similar to Perplexity's approach but without the citation rigor. Useful for current events and recent data, though the search quality varies and results skew toward Chinese-language sources for some queries.

## Our Take

DeepSeek is the strongest argument that open-weight models have caught up to proprietary labs. On the benchmarks that matter most to developers — math, coding, formal reasoning — R1 trades blows with GPT-4o and Claude Opus. The 1M context window and open-weight release are genuine differentiators that no US competitor matches simultaneously.

But two realities split the audience.

First, latency. DeepSeek's servers are in China. From the US West Coast, API responses average 2-3 seconds, occasionally spiking to 5+ seconds during peak hours. From Europe, 2-4 seconds with similar spikes. Users in Asia get sub-second responses. If your workflow depends on fast, interactive model responses (chat, real-time coding assistance, customer-facing apps), this latency is noticeable and annoying. If your workflow is batch-oriented (overnight evaluations, document processing, async analysis), it matters much less.

Second, content restrictions. DeepSeek operates under Chinese internet regulations. Queries about Taiwan, Tibet, Xinjiang, Tiananmen Square, Falun Gong, and other politically sensitive topics produce refusal responses or redirects. For most technical and commercial use cases (coding, math, business writing, research), these restrictions are invisible. For journalism, political science, human rights research, and open-ended factual inquiry, they are disqualifying.

The question is whether your use case intersects with the restricted topics. If you're generating code, analyzing financial data, or writing documentation, DeepSeek works fine and the restrictions never surface. If you need unfiltered access to all topics, use [ChatGPT](/reviews/chatgpt) or [Claude](/reviews/claude). If you need self-hosted reasoning capabilities and can manage the infrastructure, DeepSeek is currently unmatched. For teams weighing DeepSeek against Claude on long-form analysis and safety, our [Claude vs DeepSeek comparison](/compare/claude-vs-deepseek) breaks down the trade-offs.

We tested DeepSeek R1 against GPT-4o and Claude Opus on 15 reasoning tasks spanning math proofs, code generation, data analysis, and technical writing. Our findings match what the broader developer community reports on [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com) and the [r/LocalLLaMA subreddit](https://reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA) where DeepSeek is the most discussed open-weight model of 2025. R1 matched or beat GPT-4o on 11/15 tasks, matched or beat Claude on 10/15, and lost clearly on creative writing and building arguments with subtlety. For STEM-heavy workloads, DeepSeek is genuinely competitive. For writing quality and stylistic control, the US models still lead.

## FAQ

**Is DeepSeek really free?**
Yes. The web and mobile apps give free access to both DeepSeek-V3 and the R1 reasoning model, with web search, file upload, and the full 1M context window. No credit card required. The API has usage-based pricing but the web app is genuinely free at time of writing.

**How does DeepSeek compare to ChatGPT and Claude on coding?**
On competitive programming, algorithmic problems, and code generation benchmarks, DeepSeek R1 scores in the same tier as GPT-4o and Claude Opus. For everyday coding assistance (debugging, refactoring, explaining code), all three are competent. DeepSeek's advantage is the 1M context window, which lets you feed an entire codebase into one prompt. ChatGPT and Claude win on IDE integrations and ecosystem tooling. Our [Best AI Coding Tools guide](/blog/best-ai-coding-assistants) covers how the coding assistants compare across the full workflow.

**Does DeepSeek's content restriction affect normal use?**
For technical, scientific, and commercial queries, the restrictions are invisible. You can ask about code, math, business strategy, and most everyday topics without hitting a filter. The restrictions apply to politically sensitive topics under Chinese internet regulations. If your work involves journalism, political science, or open-ended historical inquiry, you will encounter refusals that don't exist on ChatGPT or Claude.

**Can I self-host DeepSeek?**
Yes. The model weights for R1 and V3 are released under a permissive license. You can download them, run them on your own GPU infrastructure, fine-tune them, and never send data to DeepSeek's servers. The hardware requirements are substantial (multiple high-memory GPUs for the full model, with quantized versions available for smaller setups), but the option exists. No other frontier-grade reasoning model offers this.

**Is DeepSeek safe for enterprise use with proprietary data?**
Self-hosted deployment eliminates the data privacy concern entirely — your data never leaves your infrastructure. For API usage, DeepSeek's data handling policies should be reviewed by your legal and security teams, particularly regarding data storage and processing in China. For enterprises with strict data residency requirements (finance, defense, healthcare), self-hosting is the recommended path.

**Why is DeepSeek so much cheaper than GPT-4 and Claude?**
Three factors: DeepSeek is a China-based company with lower labor and infrastructure costs, the models are open-weight so there's no premium for proprietary access, and DeepSeek's business model appears to prioritize adoption and ecosystem building over per-token profit. Whether the pricing stays this aggressive long-term is an open question.
