## What FaceMagic Actually Does

FaceMagic replaces faces in video clips using AI face mapping. You upload a source face photo and a target video, and FaceMagic processes the swap frame by frame. The neural network handles face detection, landmark alignment, color matching, and blending across the entire clip.

The key differentiator from Reface is custom video support. FaceMagic lets you use your own footage — dance videos, skits, reaction clips — rather than being restricted to a template library. This makes it useful for creators who film their own content and want to add face swap as a creative effect.

The mobile app is optimized for short-form vertical video (9:16 aspect ratio) with direct export to TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Processing is cloud-based, so generation speed depends on server load and internet connection rather than your phone's hardware.

Output quality depends heavily on input conditions. Matching lighting, similar face angles, and high-resolution source material produce the best results. Mismatched lighting, extreme angles, or fast motion produce visible artifacts, flickering, and tracking drift.

## vs Reface and FaceFusion

FaceMagic sits between Reface and FaceFusion in the face swap tool landscape. Reface is more polished with a larger daily-updated template library and a better mobile UX, but restricts you to its templates — you cannot upload your own video. FaceFusion is the professional desktop option with HyperSwap 256px models, real-time streaming, frame enhancement, and unlimited free usage, but requires Python, a CUDA GPU, and technical setup. FaceMagic is the middle ground: custom videos on mobile with decent quality and no desktop required.

For most mobile creators, the decision tree is simple. If Reface's templates work for your content style, use Reface — it is a more polished app. If you need custom video face swap on mobile specifically, FaceMagic is your best option. If quality matters more than convenience and you have a capable desktop, FaceFusion is the upgrade path.
