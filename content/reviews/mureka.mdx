Is Mureka good for YouTube background music? After the V9.5 update from Kunlun Wanwei, the answer is getting easier to say yes to. Mureka is the AI music generator that chose restraint over saturation: it deliberately leaves space in the arrangement, plans the song structure before generating, and claims the highest prompt-control rate in the category. For YouTube creators, the two numbers that matter are the full commercial rights on the $9 Pro tier and the clean stem separation that lets you pull out an instrumental bed without the AI smear. This review covers how V9.5 sounds, what it costs, and where it still loses to Suno.

## Key Features

- **MusiCoT (Music Chain-of-Thought)** — The headline feature of V9.5. The model plans the full song structure (intro, verse, chorus, bridge) before generating any audio, instead of improvising note by note. The result is songs with recognizable form: actual verses, actual choruses, actual endings. This is the feature that separates V9.5 from every version before it, and it is why reviewers describe the output as "music" rather than "AI audio."
- **Natural vocal delivery** — Kunlun reports a 61.0% vocal pass rate, up from the previous generation, with breath control and emotional phrasing rather than the processed, compressed singing that plagues most AI vocals. The Chinese guofeng-style improvements are the most dramatic: Mandarin pronunciation, breath handling, and harmony balance are now the best in class, per the [benchmark breakdown](https://www.tmtpost.com/8073797.html).
- **MIDI export (Premier tier)** — Unique in the AI music category. You can export a generated song as MIDI and rebuild it in Logic, Ableton, or Pro Tools. Every other AI music tool locks you into the audio file. For producers, this changes what an AI generation is: a sketch you can rework instead of a finished artifact.
- **Prompt control at 97.0%** — V9.5 follows stylistic instructions with a 97.0% pass rate and 95.7% full-style reflection, per Kunlun's numbers. In practice this means "melancholic lo-fi with a soft piano intro" produces something recognizably that, rather than a guess.
- **Voice cloning and Music Agent Studio (Premier)** — Chat-based workflow for building songs conversationally, plus voice cloning for training the generator on your own voice.

## Our Take

Mureka V9.5 is the first AI music generator that feels designed by someone who is tired of AI music. The restraint is audible: where Suno fills every frequency with polished sound, Mureka leaves blank space, and the songs breathe because of it. The MusiCoT structure planning fixes the category's biggest tell, the endless wall of sound that never resolves into a song form. On melody, vocal expressiveness, and arrangement, Kunlun's own benchmarks put V9.5 ahead of Suno 5.5 and Lyria 3.5.

But the honest read has two caveats. First, the model's English vocals are still uneven. The pass rate drops noticeably on English-language songs, with awkward stress patterns that a native ear catches immediately. If your YouTube content is English-first, test before committing. Second, the output is less "finished" than Suno's out of the box. Reviewers keep landing on the same verdict: use Suno for the song, Mureka for the stems. That is not a dismissal; stem quality and MIDI export are exactly the tools a background-music workflow needs.

For the YouTube background music use case specifically, the pricing math is clean: $9/month on Pro gets you around 500 songs with full commercial rights. Suno's Pro tier at $8/month does not include MIDI or voice cloning, and its licensing covers commercial use too, so the choice comes down to sound and workflow, not legality. If you need instrumentals that sit under a voice track without fighting it, Mureka's restraint is a feature. If you need radio-ready energy, Suno still wins. ## What Mureka Does in Practice

Kunlun Wanwei trained V9.5 on 25,000+ pieces of user feedback collected across the V8 and V9 releases, and the version history tells the story: V8 was "releasable," V9 added "fine control," and V9.5 is the version they describe as "worth repeated listening." The model launched globally on August 18, 2026, at [mureka.ai](https://mureka.ai) overseas and mureka.cn domestically.

In a generation session, the workflow is: describe the song in plain language, optionally specify style, structure, and mood, and the model plans the arrangement before synthesizing. The 61.0% vocal pass rate and 97.0% prompt-control numbers come from Kunlun's internal evaluation, with the [independent hands-on coverage](https://undetectr.com/blog/mureka-ai-review) from reviewers generally confirming the direction: better song form, cleaner vocal articulation, and dramatically better Chinese-language output.

The stems deserve special attention for YouTube use. Mureka's stem separation is cleaner than Suno's in side-by-side tests, which matters when you want to duck the music under a voiceover without audible artifacts. Combined with MIDI export on the Premier tier, a creator can take a generated song, pull out the instrumental, and rebuild the arrangement in a DAW around the video's pacing.

The weak spot remains English-language vocals. On guofeng and Mandarin-pop tracks the model is clearly best in class. On English pop and rock, the stress patterns drift, and the mixes can feel unfinished compared to Suno's polish. For creators working in English, this is the difference between a tool you use daily and a tool you reach for when the brief is instrumental.

## FAQ

**Is Mureka good for YouTube background music?**
Yes, with one condition: use the Pro tier or above, because free-tier output has no commercial rights. Pro at $9/month includes roughly 500 songs with full commercial rights, and the clean stem separation makes it easy to pull instrumentals that sit under a voice track.

**How much does Mureka cost?**
Free, Pro at $9/month (or $7/month billed annually, ~500 songs), and Premier at $27/month (or $24/month annual, ~2,000 songs plus WAV, stems, MIDI export, and voice cloning).

**Does Mureka export MIDI?**
Yes, on the Premier tier. It is the only major AI music generator with MIDI export, which lets you rebuild a generated song in Logic, Ableton, or Pro Tools.

**Is Mureka better than Suno?**
It depends on the language and the job. Mureka wins on Chinese vocals, structural control, stem separation, and MIDI export. Suno wins on English vocal warmth and out-of-the-box polish. The common reviewer verdict: use Suno for the song, Mureka for the stems.

**Is Mureka free?**
There is a free tier with MP3 output and limited generations, but it grants no commercial rights. For any monetized use, including YouTube, the Pro tier is the minimum.

**Can I use Mureka music on monetized YouTube videos?**
Yes, with Pro or Premier. Both tiers include full commercial rights. Free-tier output cannot be monetized.
