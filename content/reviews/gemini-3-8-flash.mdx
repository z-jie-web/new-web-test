Is Gemini 3.8 Flash good for coding agents? Google released 3.8 Flash on September 2, 2026, three weeks after 3.7 Flash and the third Flash update in six weeks. The model is an iteration on 3.7, not a new base model, and its benchmark story is the most aggressive workhorse positioning Google has shipped: DeepSWE v1.1 at 73.7%, within 0.3 points of Claude Opus 5, at a list price of $0.75/$3.75 per million tokens. Two caveats complicate the picture. First, 3.8 Flash burns more tokens than 3.7 to reach those scores, so real per-task costs run about 40% higher despite identical prices. Second, the intro price doubles on January 1, 2027. This review covers what the model does, what it really costs, and where it still trails the frontier.

## Key Features

- **Iteration on 3.7 Flash, not a new base model**: Google built 3.8 Flash directly on the 3.7 Flash line with a ~1M-token context window and 65,536-token max output. It is available through the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Android Studio, and Antigravity. A specialized [Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber](https://security.googleblog.com) variant ships separately through the Fairwind Program for trusted defenders, with 650+ partner organizations already enrolled.
- **Software-engineering capability near the frontier**: DeepSWE v1.1 at 73.7% (Google-reported; independent Datacurve measured 74% ±1 at high effort) versus 3.7 Flash's 65.3% and Opus 5's 74.0%. Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 89.4% edges Opus 5's 89.1%. On domain-specific agents it leads too: Vals Finance Agent v2 at 61.4% and Harvey legal-agent tests at 10.0% versus Opus 5's 58.6% and 6.7%.
- **Three thinking-intensity levels**: low, medium, and high reasoning effort are selectable per request, and the model's agent loops make iterative tool calls until the task resolves. Google's guidance is explicit: budget-conscious teams should drop the effort level or stay on 3.7 Flash rather than pay for capability they will not use.
- **Measured speed leader**: Artificial Analysis clocked ~304 output tokens per second, the fastest of 195 models in its index. For interactive agent loops and long refactors, latency matters as much as benchmark points.
- **Honest weak spots on harder general benchmarks**: Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 19.1% versus Opus 5's 51.8%, GDPval-AA v2 knowledge work at 1545 Elo versus 1824, and OSWorld 2.0 computer operation at 59.0% versus 75.4%. The AI index reads 59 at high effort against Opus 5's 63 at max: close on coding, a real gap on general agentic reasoning.

## Our Take

Gemini 3.8 Flash is the strongest argument yet that the coding-model race now has two tiers: frontier flagships and workhorses that have nearly closed the gap on software engineering specifically. The DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench 2.1 numbers (within a point of Opus 5) were flagship territory three months ago. Getting them at $0.75/$3.75 list, with ~304 tokens/second throughput, changes what a default coding model looks like for most teams.

The caveats are real, and they are about cost structure, not capability. 3.8 Flash reaches those scores by working harder: roughly 166 steps and 143K output tokens per DeepSWE task versus Opus 5's 99 steps and 118K. More output tokens at a lower rate still means per-task bills run about 40% higher than 3.7 Flash, and the intro price doubles on January 1, 2027. Google's own advice (drop the effort level, or stay on 3.7 if you only need cheaper iteration) is the most honest thing in the release notes. Meanwhile the cadence itself is a strategic question: three Flash releases in six weeks means benchmarks age faster than evaluation cycles.

The verdict: if you run coding agents at volume and want Opus-5-adjacent software engineering at workhorse prices, Gemini 3.8 Flash is currently the best per-dollar option measured, the cheapest at its intelligence level per [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai). If you need the hardest general agentic reasoning, or one model that does everything, [Claude Fable 5.1](/reviews/claude-fable-5-1) still earns its premium. ## What Gemini 3.8 Flash Does in Practice

Google shipped Gemini 3.8 Flash on September 2, 2026, three weeks after 3.7 Flash (August 13) and six weeks after 3.6 Flash (July 21). The release pattern is deliberate: Google is treating Flash as a continuously improving workhorse line rather than a yearly flagship, iterating roughly monthly. 3.8 Flash keeps the same API surface and context window as 3.7, so existing integrations move over by swapping the model name.

In practice the model behaves differently from its predecessor. It reasons longer before acting (more hidden-reasoning steps and more iterative tool calls), which is how it converts 3.7-level capability into 3.8-level results. Independent testing ([Datacurve](https://datacurve.com), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai)) confirms the direction of Google's claims: Datacurve measured DeepSWE at 74% ±1 at high effort, and Artificial Analysis named it the cheapest model at its measured intelligence level at ~$0.58 per standardized task, while ranking its output speed first among 195 models.

The speed matters in real agent work. At ~304 tokens/second, long refactors stream back fast enough to keep a human-in-the-loop workflow feeling interactive, and agent loops that would stall on slower models keep moving. The 1M context window handles repository-scale inputs in one prompt, mirroring the other frontier models.

The weaknesses show up in hands-on testing. One independent test had the model generate runnable 3D code in minutes, but the results were "runnable but wrong": crude helicopter mechanics, leaking terrain in water simulations. High benchmark scores do not yet equal reliable real-world output on every task, and the verbose loop mechanism can become a cost trap on ambiguous tasks that never converge. Google's developer guidance addresses exactly this: if cost control is the priority, lower the thinking intensity or keep 3.7 Flash.

The list price is identical to 3.7 Flash ($0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens), and that is the number most coverage quotes. The two asterisks matter more. First, this is an intro rate that doubles on January 1, 2027; teams building long-term cost models should budget $1.50/$7.50 from next year. Even with both asterisks, the model remains dramatically cheaper than the frontier flagships: one DeepSWE task averaged $2.36 versus Opus 5's $11.84 and Fable 5.1's higher still, and Artificial Analysis calls it the cheapest model at its measured intelligence level. For volume agent workloads the calculus holds; for light interactive use, 3.7 Flash or a lower effort setting is the cheaper default.

Yes, for software-engineering work specifically. DeepSWE v1.1 at 73.7% sits 0.3 points behind Claude Opus 5, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 89.4% edges it, at a list price under a tenth of the frontier flagships. It trails on harder general agent benchmarks like Terminal-Bench 4.0 and OSWorld.

**How much does Gemini 3.8 Flash cost?**
$0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output, identical to 3.7 Flash, but this intro rate doubles to $1.50/$7.50 on January 1, 2027. Because 3.8 Flash uses ~30% more output tokens per task, real per-task costs run ~40% higher than 3.7 despite the same list price.

**Gemini 3.8 Flash vs 3.7 Flash, which should I use?**
3.8 Flash for autonomous capability: DeepSWE jumped 8.4 points to 73.7% and domain-agent scores lead the frontier. 3.7 Flash for cost control: ~40% cheaper per task, same list price, and Google's own recommendation for budget teams.

**Does Gemini 3.8 Flash work with Claude Code or Cursor?**

**Is Gemini 3.8 Flash free?**
