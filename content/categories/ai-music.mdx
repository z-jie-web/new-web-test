## What to Look For

**Sound character.** Suno's output is polished, radio-ready, slightly processed. Udio's output is warmer, more dynamic, with better instrument separation. Neither is objectively better, they're different flavors. Suno sounds like a finished mix. Udio sounds like a studio recording waiting for mastering.

**Vocal quality.** Suno's vocals are more consistent across genres, especially pop and hip-hop. Udio's vocals have more natural breath and dynamics but can be less reliable in dense arrangements. If vocals are the centerpiece of your track, Suno currently wins.

**Workflow speed.** Suno generates full songs with vocals in under 30 seconds. Udio works in 30-second increments and takes longer. For rapid iteration and idea generation, Suno is faster. For precise control over specific sections, Udio's true inpainting wins.

**Legal standing.** Both tools face ongoing copyright litigation. Udio settled with Universal, Warner, and Merlin. Suno settled with Warner only. Both embed C2PA provenance metadata in exports. Neither can guarantee full copyright ownership of AI-generated output, the US Copyright Office still requires "meaningful human authorship." If you plan to commercially release AI-generated music, understand the legal risks before investing serious time.

**Download availability.** Suno allows MP3/WAV downloads on all paid tiers. Udio disabled downloads in late 2025 during its licensing transition, a critical limitation if you really need to export and use the audio.

## FAQ

**Can I copyright AI-generated music?** Not fully, as of 2026. The US Copyright Office requires "meaningful human authorship" ,  you need to substantially remix, re-record, or add human performances to claim IP. Raw AI output alone is not copyrightable.

**Which is better for background music for videos?** Suno for vocal-forward tracks. For instrumental-only background music, Stable Audio or Beatoven.ai may be better fits, they specialize in royalty-free stems and loops.

**Is AI music legal to use commercially?** It depends on the platform's license terms and your jurisdiction. Paid tiers generally grant commercial usage rights, but the underlying training data lawsuits (Sony vs. Suno, Sony vs. Udio) are still active. Check the platform's current terms before commercial use.

**How much does AI music generation cost?** Both Suno and Udio have free tiers (~10-50 credits/day). Paid plans start at ~$10/month for both. Suno Premier (~$30/month) adds voice cloning and custom models.
